## Rule

A distribution platform is a **revenue endpoint**, not the business.

```text
Business Blueprint
→ classify asset type
→ select all compatible worthwhile channels
→ publish compliantly
→ attribute traffic + sales + fees + support
→ compare contribution by channel
→ double down / syndicate / retire
```

The goal is **distribution breadth without indiscriminate duplication**.

## Diversification controls

### Do

- maintain one canonical product/version source;
- adapt listing copy and packaging to each platform's buyer intent;
- preserve identical core promise and evidence claims;
- use channel-specific tracking and discount codes/UTMs where possible;
- calculate contribution after the exact platform fee and support burden;
- preserve customer ownership/contact rights where terms permit;
- retain an owned landing page/storefront so marketplace demand is not the only acquisition source.

### Do not

- sign up to a platform because it exists;
- assume one listing format is compliant everywhere;
- duplicate AI-generated spam across marketplaces;
- rely on a marketplace's internal discovery without measuring it;
- make Whop royalty/Partner economics a prerequisite for Business Blueprint viability;
- let one channel become an unmeasured single point of failure.

## Platform concentration metric

Track:

`largest channel contribution / total Blueprint contribution`

As revenue develops, DRF should know:

- % sales by platform;
- % contribution by platform;
- % traffic by platform;
- owned/direct %;
- platform-specific recurring/royalty %;
- refund/dispute rate by platform;
- support minutes by platform;
- buyer activation/deployment by platform.

A dominant channel can be economically rational. The risk is **unexamined dependence**, not concentration by itself.

## Whop treatment

Whop is a high-priority channel because its current digital-business orientation, marketplace, agentic tooling and channel-specific recurring economics can make it unusually valuable.

Current channel evidence now distinguishes:

- first-party-verified Blueprint programme mechanics;
- first-party-verified separate Partner economics;
- unproven live DRF payout attribution/stacking;
- unproven representative conversion, payout distribution and downstream retention.

The correct formula remains:

`Business Blueprint economics + Whop-specific upside`

not:

`Business Blueprint = Whop`

Whop evidence lives under `channels/whop/`.

## Website and launch kits as a product type

A website/revenue launch kit is one **Business Blueprint SKU format**. If the Blueprint contains native Framer or Webflow assets, those specialist marketplaces can be added to the general channel portfolio.

This is a routing rule, not a second business opportunity:

`proven Outcome × Niche → Business Blueprint → website/launch-kit product format when useful → compatible channels → optional implementation → more proof`
