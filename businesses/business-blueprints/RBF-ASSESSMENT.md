### 1 September score decision

Current first-party Whop programme verification improves evidence quality for one important channel, but it does **not** yet change the parent RBS.

Why the RBS remains **82/100**:

- parent viability is deliberately multi-platform and does not depend on Whop recurring economics;
- representative buyer conversion and channel contribution are still unproven;
- buyer deployment/activation and support burden remain unproven;
- live DRF royalty/Partner attribution and downstream retention remain unproven.

A stronger single channel can improve the portfolio without mechanically increasing the parent score.

### First test channels

The first SKU should normally include:

1. **one marketplace/discovery channel** — Whop or another product-fit marketplace;
2. **one independent/hybrid storefront** — Gumroad, Payhip, Lemon Squeezy, Shopify or Contra depending product type;
3. **one specialist marketplace** only when the asset has a native form such as Notion, Framer, Webflow, PromptBase or API/software.

Do not choose three channels merely to satisfy a number. Product fit governs.

## 6. Forward-test economics

### Capital cap

**Up to $3,000 total.**

This includes:

- product packaging/QA;
- marketplace/store setup;
- creative/listing assets;
- small paid acquisition experiments if justified;
- support allowance;
- analytics/tracking;
- required platform/tool costs.

### Core viability hurdle

The parent Business Blueprint should aim for:

- **≥30% contribution after attributable acquisition cost**;
- refund/credit rate **<10%**;
- support burden low enough to preserve digital-product leverage.

Each channel must be calculated separately:

`sale price - channel fees - refunds - support - attributable CAC = channel contribution`

### P3 pass

Within the bounded test:

- at least **5 genuine paid buyers/deposits**;
- at least **2 buyers actually intending to deploy/use** the Blueprint;
- contribution hurdle met on the total direct-sale cohort;
- no fatal rights/platform-policy issue;
- traffic and conversion attributable by channel;
- enough evidence to decide which channels deserve more listing/marketing effort.

A channel can fail while the parent business passes.

## 10. Risks

- low-quality generic Blueprint becomes commodity content;
- buyer buys but never deploys;
- product claims overstate underlying proof;
- piracy/copying;
- different platform packaging/support requirements erode leverage;
- direct marketplace traffic proves weaker than expected;
- one channel becomes dominant and introduces concentration risk;
- support/customisation silently turns the product into an agency service;
- platform-specific recurring economics change.

## 11. Decision

**FORWARD TEST.**

Business Blueprints has a stronger architecture than the Whop-only framing because it can route one proven asset across multiple demand and checkout surfaces while retaining Whop-specific upside.

However, the score does not unlock scale capital. DRF still needs real buyers, contribution and activation evidence.
