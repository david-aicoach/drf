## 1. Business definition

A Business Blueprint is a reusable package of operating IP that lets a buyer deploy/run a known business or revenue system faster than starting from zero.

Possible components:

- operating playbook;
- customer/niche definition;
- pricing model;
- sales scripts;
- SOPs/checklists;
- CRM/workflow templates;
- website/landing-page templates;
- automation recipes;
- prompts/agent skills;
- dashboards/scorecards;
- onboarding assets;
- implementation instructions;
- financial model/test gates;
- evidence and limitations.

The asset should be built from **real DRF proof**, not generic business-plan content.

| **Whop Blueprints** | Deployable digital businesses with copied products, pricing and website | Current first-party Blueprints guide explicitly treats Blueprints as running businesses and states creators receive 10% of Whop's profit on attributed downstream sales | Strong channel-level confirmation that deployable packaged business systems are now a first-class marketplace product format |

### Counter-evidence

The market is proven but uneven:

- thousands of marketplace views/free downloads can still produce weak paid conversion;
- leading sellers often benefit from owned audiences, SEO/content or ecosystem visibility;
- repeatable delivery does not guarantee repeatable acquisition;
- platform fees, processing, payouts, support, refunds and policy vary materially by channel;
- generic templates are easy to copy and commoditise;
- buyers can purchase but fail to activate/deploy the product.

### Transferability boundary

EMP3 proves:

- buyers pay for reusable systems/templates;
- one system can sell repeatedly at high gross margin;
- recurring and upsell models are viable;
- marketplaces, owned content and specialist ecosystems can acquire buyers.

EMP3 does **not** prove:

- DRF's exact buyer and price;
- DRF's CAC by channel;
- activation/deployment quality;
- support/refund burden;
- contribution;
- repeatability for the selected Blueprint.

That is why EMP3 does not become EMP4 yet.

- live DRF payout basis, attribution duration, refund/chargeback treatment and stacking remain execution questions;
- representative Blueprint creator-income distribution and downstream retention remain unavailable.

Whop-specific evidence remains under `channels/whop/`.

### Gumroad

Current first-party economics:

- direct/profile-link sales: **10% + US$0.50** plus applicable processing;
- Discover sales: **30%**;
- automatic high-volume direct-sales discount to **5% + US$0.50 for new direct sales after monthly paid sales reach US$20,000**, resetting each calendar month.

The discount is **conditional scale upside** and must not be used in the current low-volume P3 test model.

### Contra Digital Products

Contra supports one-time products, subscriptions, merchant-of-record handling and a strong product-to-service bridge.

As of 3 September, exact digital-product fees remain a **first-party source conflict**:

- current pricing page: Free product fee capped 5% (min US$3 / max US$29), Pro no product fee;
- current Help/product docs: price-band Non-Pro/Pro product fees plus processing.

DRF must verify the live seller-account/checkout fee immediately before launch and model conservatively until then.

### Notion Marketplace

Current English first-party seller documentation now states:

- merchant-of-record model;
- **8% + US$0.40 per transaction**;
- creators outside the US can pay **+1% FX** when payout currency is converted;
- **14-day** payout eligibility hold;
- paid templates require approval and commercially reasonable creator support;
- **United Arab Emirates is currently listed as eligible** for direct Marketplace selling/payment onboarding.

This supersedes the active 1–2 September DRF interpretation of 10% + US$0.40. An older localized Notion Help surface still exposes 10%, so the fee and seller terms must be verified again immediately before launch.

Best use remains Notion-native CRM/OS/database/workflow components.

### Lemon Squeezy

Base fee remains **5% + US$0.50** with merchant-of-record infrastructure.

The fully loaded cost can also include:

- +1.5% international payments;
- +1.5% PayPal;
- +0.5% subscription payments;
- payout and optional marketing/affiliate/recovery fees depending on method.

For UAE economics, model the actual payment/payout mix rather than treating 5% + US$0.50 as universal all-in cost.

### Payhip

Current first-party pricing remains:

- Free: **5%**;
- Plus: **US$29/month + 2%**;
- Pro: **US$99/month + 0%**;
- processor charges separate.

### Shopify / owned commerce / agentic distribution

Shopify remains important as the owned commerce layer. Current Agentic Storefronts evidence now makes the channel map more explicit:

- eligible catalogue products can be exposed to **ChatGPT, Google AI Mode/Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Meta**;
- products are supplied through Shopify Catalog, or the Google & YouTube / Merchant Center route for Google AI Mode and Gemini;
- ChatGPT currently acts as a discovery/referrer surface and buyers complete checkout through the merchant's Shopify checkout;
- supported Google AI Mode/Gemini, Copilot and Meta flows can use Shopify-powered direct checkout when enabled;
- the merchant retains the customer relationship and post-purchase experience;
- Shopify admin retains channel/referrer attribution.

This is stronger infrastructure evidence, but **not DRF Blueprint conversion proof**. Live use also requires acceptance of Shopify's supplemental Agentic Storefronts terms, which remains a founder-approval boundary.

### Framer / Webflow

Current first-party evidence remains strong:

- Framer creators keep **100%** of direct paid Marketplace product revenue and eligible templates can earn referral commission;
- Webflow advertises **95% creator payout** on template sales.

Use only when the Blueprint contains genuine website/template assets.

### PromptBase / developer channels

Current PromptBase support states:

- 20% marketplace fee;
- 0% platform fee through seller referral links;
- 10% custom-job fee.

Use specialist prompt/agent/software/API marketplaces only where the Blueprint contains a genuine specialist component.

## 5. Product-marketplace fit matters more than maximum listing count

The strategy is **multi-platform, not indiscriminate**.

A complete HVAC Enquiry-to-Revenue Blueprint might use Whop + Gumroad + Contra/owned checkout. If it includes a Notion OS or Framer template, those components can additionally use the native specialist marketplace.

Do not force every asset onto every marketplace.

## 11. Revised Next Proof — test only remaining uncertainty

Because EMP3 proves the broad reusable-system/template category, the next P3 test asks:

> **Can DRF sell the selected proof-backed Blueprint to the intended buyer at the pre-committed price through a repeatable channel, and does at least one buyer genuinely intend to deploy it without excessive support?**

Recommended P3 threshold remains:

- choose one launch price within **US$199–399** before traffic;
- obtain **2 unrelated paid buyers/deposits** at that price or equivalent founder-approved commercial commitments;
- at least **1** buyer reaches genuine deployment/activation intent;
- at least **1** buyer comes through a channel that is not merely a direct personal favour/relationship;
- measure source, conversion, fees, attributable CAC where possible, refund signal and expected support;
- no fatal rights/platform issue.

P4 then requires actual paid delivery/activation and actual cost capture.

## 12. Main risks

- generic/low-proof Blueprints become commodity information products;
- piracy/copying;
- different platform listings create version drift;
- fragmented support channels increase service time;
- marketplace traffic does not convert;
- direct traffic remains expensive;
- buyer deployment/activation is poor;
- platform-specific fee/policy surfaces change or conflict across locales/documents;
- base fee assumptions understate processing/payout/subscription economics;
- implementation silently becomes bespoke consulting.
