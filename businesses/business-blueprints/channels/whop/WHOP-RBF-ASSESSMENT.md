# 5. Direct-sale backtest

## Published Whop payment cost

For domestic cards Whop currently publishes:

```text
2.7% + $0.30 per successful transaction
```

International cards can add 1.5%; currency conversion can add another 1%.

There is no general monthly seller subscription required for payment processing.

## Price evidence

Observed paid comparable Whop products support a broad range. For the first test, use **$199** because a current `Digital Business Blueprint` is already listed at that price and it is high enough to test meaningful willingness to pay without requiring enterprise sales.

### Three-price stress test

The table below solves for the **maximum CAC that still leaves a 30% contribution margin after processing, a refund reserve and support cost**.

The 30% contribution hurdle is a **DRF test standard**, not a Whop market fact.

| Case | Sale price | Processing assumption | Refund reserve | Support cost / sale | Desired contribution | **Maximum CAC** | Read |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Downside** | **$99** | 5.2% + $0.30 | 10% | $30 | 30% of sale | **~$24** | Fragile. This price only works with cheap/organic acquisition or lower support. |
| **Base** | **$199** | 2.7% + $0.30 | 5% | $20 | 30% of sale | **~$104** | Testable. Paid acquisition can work if CAC is kept around $100 or below. |
| **Upside** | **$499** | 2.7% + $0.30 | 3% | $10 | 30% of sale | **~$311** | Strong room for acquisition, but pricing power must be proven. |

### Base-case sale economics at $199

```text
Sale price                              $199.00
Domestic processing (2.7% + $0.30)      -$5.67
5% refund reserve                        -$9.95
Support allowance                        -$20.00
CAC test ceiling                         -$100.00
-----------------------------------------------
Contribution after CAC                    $63.38
Contribution margin                         31.8%
```

This is a **test hurdle**, not an achieved margin.

### Cash sensitivity at the base hurdle

Assuming the same $199 price and $100 CAC ceiling:

| Paid sales / month | Contribution after CAC | Monthly cash-on-$3K-test-capital before fixed overhead | Meaning |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **5** | **~$317** | **10.6%** | Demand exists but economics are still small. |
| **10** | **~$634** | **21.1%** | Useful early signal. |
| **20** | **~$1,268** | **42.3%** | Attractive if repeatable and support remains bounded. |
| **40** | **~$2,535** | **84.5%** | Strong digital-product economics if sustainable. |

These figures are **not a forecast**. They are a sensitivity table and exclude fixed overhead, royalties and Partner commissions.

At these base assumptions, approximately **48 sales** would recover a full $3,000 test investment from direct-sale contribution alone. Royalty/Partner income would reduce that payback if it is actually earned.

# 7. P3 forward-test design

## Hypothesis

> A proof-backed business Blueprint can sell at **$199+** to entrepreneurs on/around Whop while leaving at least **30% contribution after acquisition and direct support costs**.

## Offer

One Blueprint only. Choose a DRF business system where we can explain:

- exactly what is sold;
- buyer/niche;
- startup requirements;
- revenue model;
- acquisition recipe;
- delivery recipe;
- KPIs;
- risks;
- evidence/proof level.

## Acquisition channels

Test a small controlled mix:

1. Whop product/store listing;
2. Whop marketplace/Blueprint discovery where available;
3. direct niche outreach;
4. affiliates/creator partners;
5. SEO/content;
6. a bounded paid-ad test.

Whop Ads is a potentially powerful rail but current official docs still describe access as beta/limited, so it is not required for the first test.

## P3 pass thresholds

All must be measured:

- **at least 5 genuine paid buyers/deposits** at $199 or above;
- **CAC ≤ $104** under the base cost assumptions, or a documented organic equivalent;
- **post-CAC contribution ≥30%** on the direct sale;
- **refund/credit rate <10%** during the test window;
- support/activation burden recorded per buyer;
- at least **2 buyers intend to deploy/use** the business system, not merely consume information;
- no fatal Whop terms/attribution constraint.

## P3 fail/recycle triggers

- no paid demand after the pre-defined qualified sample;
- CAC materially above the break-even ceiling;
- buyers only value the content as a cheap course/PDF rather than a deployable business asset;
- support becomes bespoke consulting;
- Whop terms materially weaken the expected recurring income;
- $3,000 or 80 founder hours are reached without a credible path to P4.

# 8. P4 revenue-proof requirements

To become **P4 Revenue Proven**, the first Whop Blueprint must show:

1. genuine cash collected;
2. product/Blueprint delivered;
3. buyer activates/deploys it;
4. first customer value observed;
5. actual CAC recorded;
6. actual processing/refunds/support recorded;
7. actual contribution calculated;
8. founder time recorded;
9. royalty/referral attribution checked separately.

One sale is enough for P4 only if payment + delivery + first value are real. It is **not** enough for P5 repeatability.

# 9. Recurring-income validation

The long-term attraction is not merely selling a $199 product.

The important compounding model is:

```text
upfront Blueprint sales
+
Blueprint business royalties
+
Whop Partner commissions
```

## What must be captured live

For every downstream business:

- Blueprint attribution;
- qualifying business activity;
- royalty amount;
- Whop gross-profit basis;
- Partner attribution;
- Partner commission;
- whether royalty + Partner commission stack;
- downstream business 30/60/90-day activity;
- downstream sales/revenue where visible;
- support minutes.

Until these are captured, **royalties and Partner income are excluded from the core direct-sale viability model**.
