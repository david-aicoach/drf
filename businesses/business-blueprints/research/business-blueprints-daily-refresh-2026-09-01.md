## 2. Counter-evidence / failure modes

The category is proven, but outcomes are highly unequal.

### Marketplace traffic does not equal willingness to pay

One current small Notion creator publicly reported roughly:

- **6,335 Marketplace views**;
- **2,996 free downloads**;
- only **93 paid sales** and **$795 total revenue** over about ten months.

The creator's own conclusion was that traction/downloads were repeatedly mistaken for conversion readiness.

This is weak individual evidence, but useful negative evidence: generic templates and marketplace visibility do not automatically create a strong business.

Source:
- https://www.reddit.com/r/notioncreations/comments/1t62sll/ive_built_12_notion_templates_and_sold_them_for_a/

### Audience advantage is real

Thomas Frank and Easlo both have material owned audiences/content engines. Their revenue proves willingness to pay and scalable delivery, but does **not** prove DRF can acquire customers at the same CAC without equivalent distribution assets.

### Standardisation can coexist with acquisition weakness

Business Done Better shows strong repeatable delivery and close to 200 customers but explicitly acknowledges the absence of a repeatable marketing engine. This reinforces DRF's need to test **distribution/CAC**, not the broad existence of demand for packaged systems.

### Support, copying and platform risk remain real

- Notion requires ongoing commercially reasonable support for paid templates.
- Different marketplaces have different refund, fee, approval and AI-content rules.
- Template/snapshot IP remains copyable even where platform IP protection exists.
- Product quality and proof matter because generic content is commoditised.

### Initial commercial test band

Current external anchors support a bounded self-serve test around:

> **US$199–399 one-time** for the core self-serve Blueprint, with optional implementation/customisation priced separately and channel-specific referral/royalty economics excluded from core viability.

This is a **test range / inference**, not a proven DRF price.

Basis:

- current Whop Blueprint-style offers at $197–199;
- Easlo products at $39–79 and a $389 lifetime bundle;
- Business Done Better recurring system at $199/month;
- premium system templates demonstrating much larger annual revenue at product prices around the low hundreds.

### Revised Next Proof — smaller and more specific

Because EMP3 already proves the broad category, DRF should **not** require five buyers merely to prove that reusable systems can sell.

The next P3 test should answer the remaining transferability question:

> **Can DRF sell the selected proof-backed Blueprint to the intended buyer at the pre-committed test price through a repeatable channel, and does at least one buyer actually intend to deploy it without excessive support?**

Recommended P3 pass threshold:

- pre-commit one launch price inside the **US$199–399** test band;
- obtain **2 unrelated paid buyers/deposits** at that price or a founder-approved equivalent commercial commitment;
- at least **1 buyer reaches genuine deployment/activation intent**;
- at least **1 buyer comes through a channel that is not merely a direct personal favour/relationship**;
- measure source, conversion, fees, CAC where attributable, refund signal and expected support;
- no fatal rights/platform issue.

P4 then requires actual paid delivery/activation and actual cost capture.

This follows the new DRF recipe: **do not re-prove externally established category demand; test only DRF-specific price/channel/activation transferability.**
