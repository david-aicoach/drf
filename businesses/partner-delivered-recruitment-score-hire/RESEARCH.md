# Research — Partner-Delivered Recruitment / Score Hire Model

Updated: 2026-08-29  
Issue: #45  
Research status: **Comprehensive commercial-model research complete; partner quality and margin must be proven on a live role**

## Executive conclusion

**Recommendation: retain at 85/100 and move it up the execution queue as a fast-cash, low-build model—not as a software opportunity.**

The model uses Talent Bridge for the high-value client interface, role calibration, candidate quality standard, assessment/evaluation and final shortlist narrative while a vetted delivery partner performs most sourcing and recruiter execution. This increases leverage without requiring Talent Bridge to build a large internal delivery team.

The model is strategically useful because it can monetise existing employer relationships immediately and simultaneously generate evidence, candidate data, role patterns and workflow lessons for the Recruitment OS, Assessment Platform and Talent Intelligence opportunities.

The risks are commercial and operational rather than technical: partner quality, candidate ownership, data handling, non-circumvention, fee splits, replacement obligations, client experience and who carries the cost when a search fails.

## Buyer problem

Employers want reliable shortlist quality without managing multiple agencies. Talent Bridge may have the client trust and evaluation capability but not always want to carry the full sourcing workload internally. A delivery partner can convert fixed internal capacity into variable fulfilment cost.

## Product definition

> **Talent Bridge owns the client, brief, quality bar and decision-ready shortlist; an approved partner performs bounded sourcing/delivery under a documented SLA.**

This is not simply referring a vacancy to another agency. Talent Bridge must remain accountable for the client-facing standard.

## Operating model

```text
client brief + commercial terms
       ↓
Talent Bridge role calibration / scorecard
       ↓
approved partner sourcing + first-stage execution
       ↓
Talent Bridge evidence/quality gate
       ↓
assessment / recruiter interview where required
       ↓
client-ready shortlist + evaluation
       ↓
interview / offer / placement
       ↓
partner settlement + quality review
```

## Partner contract requirements

At minimum:

- client ownership/non-solicitation;
- candidate ownership and duplicate-candidate rules;
- permitted data use and confidentiality;
- response/SLA expectations;
- minimum evidence required with submissions;
- fee split and payment trigger;
- replacement/refund allocation;
- candidate consent and privacy responsibility;
- communication rules with client/candidate;
- quality-remediation process;
- termination and handover.

## Commercial model

Use Talent Bridge's approved recruitment fee structure with an explicit partner fulfilment share. The customer should receive one Talent Bridge agreement and one accountable service standard.

The partner percentage should be calculated from actual delivery responsibility rather than an arbitrary 50/50 split.

Track per search:

```text
client fee received
- partner share
- assessments/licences
- Talent Bridge consultant time
- marketing/source cost
- replacement/exception reserve
= gross contribution
```

## Why this matters to DRF

This offer can create immediate revenue while building reusable assets:

- role scorecards;
- screening packs;
- candidate evidence structures;
- assessment benchmarks;
- partner performance data;
- client hiring signals;
- candidate salary/availability data;
- repeatable recruitment OS workflows.

It is therefore a useful **commercial proving ground**, even if its pure MRR score is lower than SaaS models.

## Best initial use

- roles Talent Bridge can calibrate strongly but does not want to source end-to-end;
- clients with trusted existing relationship;
- searches with clear fee economics;
- one delivery partner per role/category initially to preserve accountability.

Avoid difficult speculative searches with weak client commitment during the pilot.

## GTM

Use existing Talent Bridge clients and inbound roles. Do not market the partner layer; sell the same Talent Bridge quality promise with stronger delivery capacity.

## Defensibility

- client relationships;
- Talent Bridge evaluation/reporting standard;
- role calibration IP;
- partner performance history;
- quality-control workflow;
- ability to attach assessments and hiring intelligence;
- eventual partner network with measured niche strengths.

## Risks

- inconsistent partner submissions;
- partner contacts client directly;
- candidate duplication/ownership disputes;
- partner mishandles candidate data or brand experience;
- unclear replacement liability;
- low margin after partner share;
- partner becomes capacity bottleneck;
- Talent Bridge still spends too much time rescuing weak delivery.

## Evidence discipline

### Verified

- Talent Bridge already has recruitment clients, commercial documents, evaluation capability and candidate ecosystem.
- Partner delivery removes the need to hire fixed internal sourcing capacity before revenue.

### DRF judgement

- This is a leverage/business-model opportunity, not a technology product.
- Talent Bridge must retain the client-facing quality gate.
- It should be tested before further recruitment software build.

### Unproven

- partner SLA compliance;
- shortlist quality;
- gross margin after partner share;
- client perception/retention;
- placement velocity.

## Validation experiment

Run one live role with one vetted partner and a written mini-SLA.

Measure:

- time to first qualified submission;
- submissions accepted/rejected by Talent Bridge;
- shortlist acceptance by client;
- interviews and placement;
- partner response time;
- Talent Bridge rescue/rework hours;
- gross contribution;
- candidate/client complaints;
- repeat-role intent.

### Pass gate

Scale only if quality remains at Talent Bridge standard, partner rework is bounded and the placement economics materially improve consultant leverage.

## Ranking implication

**Keep 85/100 structurally, but increase execution priority.** It has weaker recurring economics than software/data products but a much shorter path to cash and generates evidence for several higher-leverage Talent Bridge products.
