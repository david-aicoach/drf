## Counter-evidence / transferability limits

1. **Direct local substitution is unusually strong.** Fidelia already offers the clinic-specific UAE/DHA product at AED499–899/month. A generic "reviews + Maps" service is not differentiated.
2. **Low-cost software compresses willingness to pay for commodity fulfilment.** NiceJob starts at US$75/month; HighLevel and other CRMs also include review workflows.
3. **Attribution remains difficult.** Review velocity, rating and Maps visibility can improve without clean proof that incremental bookings or accepted treatments came from the intervention.
4. **Healthcare responses carry privacy/compliance risk.** Public responses must not confirm patient identity or disclose clinical information; sensitive complaints require human escalation.
5. **Google policy risk is real.** Google prohibits fake or incentivised engagement and requires contributions to reflect genuine experiences. The product cannot use review gating, incentives or coached/fabricated review content.
6. **Google local rankings are not controllable.** Relevance, distance and prominence jointly determine local results; no legitimate operator can promise a fixed rank.

Google source: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114

1. **DRF acquisition / local price acceptance** for the attached service rather than SaaS;

External proof can support P1/P2 only when the selected Business × Niche commercial design is sufficiently underwritten. It cannot award P3–P6.
