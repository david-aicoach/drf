# Revenue Blueprint Scoring and Investment Readiness

**Status:** Canonical guideline  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 31 August 2026  
**Governing issue:** #60  
**Priority:** Digital businesses first; extend with separate model cards for traditional businesses later.

## Purpose

DRF is the Revenue Factory. Its job is to find, test, improve and package businesses that can produce cash, profit and durable owner value.

A business must never be called investable, proven or Blueprint-ready because it has a high desk-research score.

Every opportunity must display four separate outputs:

```text
1. REVENUE BLUEPRINT SCORE /100
How strong could this business be?

2. PROOF LEVEL P0-P6
How much has actually been verified?

3. INVESTMENT GATE
What decision is allowed now?

4. RETURN PROFILE
How much capital and founder time go in, and what monthly/annual cash may come out?
```

## One-line decision format

Use this format wherever an opportunity is summarised:

> **Revenue Blueprint Score: 82/100 · Proof: P1 Desk Underwritten · Gate: TEST ONLY · Capital unlocked: up to $3,000 · Investor-ready: No**

Never show a standalone score without proof and gate labels.

---

# 1. Revenue Blueprint Score — 0 to 100

The Revenue Blueprint Score measures the **structural quality and revenue-producing potential** of a business. It is comparable across business models, but it is not a probability of success.

Score every factor from 0 to 10 and multiply by its weight.

| Factor | Weight | Founder question |
|---|---:|---|
| **Demand, market and timing** | **15** | Are enough buyers actively looking for this now, and is the timing favourable? |
| **Pain, willingness to pay and pricing power** | **10** | Is the problem expensive or urgent enough for buyers to move money? |
| **Revenue quality and retention** | **10** | Is revenue repeatable, recurring, expanding or naturally repeat-purchased? |
| **Unit economics and margin** | **15** | Does each sale/customer produce attractive gross and contribution profit after real delivery costs? |
| **Customer acquisition and paid growth** | **10** | Can customers be reached predictably, including through ads, with acceptable CAC and payback? |
| **Delivery repeatability and customer outcome** | **10** | Can the same offer reliably create the promised result without bespoke reinvention? |
| **Scalability, leverage and founder independence** | **10** | Can revenue grow faster than labour, cost and founder time? |
| **Capital efficiency, payback and ROI** | **10** | How quickly and strongly does invested cash return? |
| **Durability, moat and concentration** | **5** | Can the business remain valuable despite competitors, platform changes and customer concentration? |
| **Operational, legal and platform risk** | **5** | How low and manageable are failure, compliance, dependency and downside risks? Higher is better. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

`weighted points = factor score ÷ 10 × factor weight`

`Revenue Blueprint Score = sum of weighted points`

## Score bands

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **85–100** | Structurally exceptional |
| **75–84** | Structurally strong |
| **65–74** | Testable but conditional |
| **50–64** | Weak unless a specific advantage changes the case |
| **Below 50** | Reject or park |

A score band controls **interest**, not investment.

## Scoring anchors

Use these simple anchors for every factor:

- **0–2:** evidence materially contradicts the business case.
- **3–4:** weak, difficult or heavily dependent on unproven assumptions.
- **5–6:** plausible but ordinary, mixed or uncertain.
- **7–8:** strong evidence or strong structural advantage.
- **9–10:** exceptional, verified advantage with few realistic substitutes or constraints.

### Evidence discipline

A factor may score highly on structural evidence while the business remains P1. The Proof Level prevents potential from being confused with performance.

Every factor must link to:

- the evidence used;
- its date;
- its evidence class;
- the most important counter-evidence;
- the assumption still being made.

# 2. Proof Level — P0 to P6

Proof Level is the business equivalent of a readiness level. It states how far the opportunity has moved from idea to reproducible business.

| Level | Name | Minimum evidence | What may be claimed |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **P0** | **Idea** | Clear proposition only | Idea captured |
| **P1** | **Desk Underwritten** | Buyer, offer, revenue streams, market, competitors, current pricing evidence, risks and initial financial model | Structurally researched |
| **P2** | **Backtested** | Historical/comparable evidence replayed through downside/base/upside assumptions; critical sensitivities and break-even identified | Economics survive a documented historical/comparable stress test |
| **P3** | **Forward Tested** | Current real-market demand test against a pre-written hypothesis, metric and pass/fail threshold | Test-ready demand signal exists |
| **P4** | **Revenue Proven** | At least one genuine payment plus delivery/activation and first-value evidence | Someone paid and received value |
| **P5** | **Repeatable** | Same core offer acquired and delivered successfully across at least three independent customers, cohorts or commercial cycles with positive contribution and bounded founder effort | A repeatable small business exists |
| **P6** | **Scale Proven / Blueprint Certified** | Repeatable acquisition, delivery, margin, retention and capacity at meaningful volume; operating package reproducible by another competent operator | Scale-ready and sellable as an evidence-backed Blueprint |

## Proof rules

- **Interviews are not revenue.** They can move P1 to P3 only when they test a defined commercial hypothesis.
- **Letters of intent are weaker than deposits.** Deposits are weaker than completed paid delivery.
- **One sale proves payment, not repeatability.**
- **Three customers are a minimum pattern check, not statistical certainty.** High-volume products should use meaningful cohorts rather than three transactions.
- **A founder manually rescuing every delivery does not prove repeatability.**
- **A profitable organic channel does not prove paid growth.**
- **A P6 Blueprint must disclose its evidence period, sample size, exclusions and known failure modes.**

## Evidence classes

Use these labels:

1. **E0 — Assumption:** founder/agent belief.
2. **E1 — Published evidence:** first-party, independent or market data.
3. **E2 — Backtest evidence:** historical or comparable data tested against the model.
4. **E3 — Forward-test evidence:** current market behaviour before full delivery.
5. **E4 — Live operating evidence:** payments, delivery, customer outcomes and real costs.
6. **E5 — Repeated/cohort evidence:** multiple customers/cycles with retention and contribution.
7. **E6 — Verified scale evidence:** stable operating results at volume, with reconciled records where practical.

Every important number must show its evidence class.

# 3. Investment Gate

The gate controls what decision and capital commitment are allowed now.

| Gate | Decision | Typical proof | Allowed action |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **REJECT** | Stop | Any | Fatal demand, economics, legality or strategic flaw |
| **RESEARCH** | Remove one major uncertainty | P0–P1 | Desk work only; no meaningful build/spend |
| **TEST** | Test demand and pricing | P1–P2 | Release Tier 1 capital only |
| **PILOT** | Prove payment and delivery | P3 | Complete a paid pilot with bounded scope |
| **FUND** | Build the repeatable small business | P4 | Release Tier 2 capital |
| **SCALE** | Increase acquisition/capacity | P5 | Release Tier 3 capital only when unit economics and capacity pass |
| **BLUEPRINT CERTIFIED** | Package and sell the operating system | P6 | Publish proof-backed Blueprint and continue measuring live cohorts |

A gate decision must be one of:

```text
GO · KILL · HOLD · RECYCLE
```

- **GO:** next gate and capital tranche may proceed.
- **KILL:** stop and preserve the lesson/evidence.
- **HOLD:** external timing/dependency blocks action.
- **RECYCLE:** change one material assumption, offer, niche or channel and retest.

# 4. Three default capital tiers

Use staged capital. Each tier buys evidence, not activity.

| Tier | Capital | Cumulative capital | Purpose | Proof target | Capital is not unlocked until |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Validate** | **$3,000** | **$3,000** | Build the smallest sellable offer, test price/channel and obtain real forward-test or paid-pilot evidence | P3–P4 | P1 underwriting and P2 backtest pass |
| **Tier 2 — Launch** | **+$7,000** | **$10,000** | Acquire and serve enough customers to prove repeatability and positive contribution | P5 | At least one paid successful delivery and no fatal unit-economics issue |
| **Tier 3 — Scale** | **+$20,000** | **$30,000** | Scale the proven channel, automate delivery, add capacity and working-capital reserve | P6 | P5 repeatability, measured CAC/payback, delivery capacity and downside survival |

The amounts are default comparison tiers, not compulsory spending targets. A business that can prove the gate for less should spend less.

## Use-of-funds rule

Every tier must state:

- exact use of funds;
- founder hours required;
- evidence/milestone purchased;
- maximum loss;
- stop date;
- next capital unlock condition.

Do not release capital to “build the platform” when the unresolved risk is demand, pricing or distribution.

# 5. Return Profile

Every opportunity must include downside, base and upside cases with a 12-month monthly forecast.

## Required outputs

- startup capital and staged capital;
- monthly revenue by revenue stream;
- gross profit;
- contribution before and after CAC;
- monthly net cash flow;
- cumulative cash position;
- break-even revenue and break-even month;
- cash runway and maximum cash loss;
- CAC and CAC payback;
- retention/repeat purchase/churn;
- monthly cash-on-cash ROI;
- year-one ROI;
- founder hours per month;
- cash generated per founder hour;
- employment-replacement month against the chosen monthly owner-income target.

## Core formulas

```text
Contribution per sale
= sale price
- refunds/discounts
- payment/platform fees
- variable fulfilment
- variable support/recovery cost

Contribution after CAC
= contribution per sale - CAC

Break-even sales per month
= fixed monthly costs ÷ contribution per sale before fixed costs

Break-even ROAS
= 1 ÷ contribution margin before advertising

Monthly cash-on-cash ROI
= monthly net cash flow ÷ cumulative capital invested

Year-one ROI
= total year-one net cash flow ÷ cumulative capital invested

Founder hourly return
= monthly net cash flow ÷ founder hours
```

Use cash rather than revenue for investor return calculations. Show the calculation basis beside every return figure.

## Investor-readiness hard gate

A business is **not investor-ready** unless all of the following are present:

- P4 or higher;
- an identified paying customer and verified revenue stream;
- delivered customer value or activation evidence;
- measured or tightly bounded contribution margin;
- 12-month monthly forecast with downside/base/upside;
- use of funds tied to the next proof milestone;
- maximum downside and runway;
- founder-time requirement;
- critical legal/platform terms verified or explicitly gated;
- one acquisition path with real evidence;
- no hidden assumption presented as fact.

A business is **not scale-investment-ready** before P5.

# 6. Backtest, forward test and live proof

## Business backtest

A backtest replays the proposed business against historical, comparable or existing operating data.

Test at least:

- demand volume;
- price and conversion;
- refund/churn/retention;
- CAC or traffic cost;
- payment/platform/royalty/commission terms;
- fulfilment and human time;
- gross and contribution margin;
- founder dependency;
- downside cash loss;
- sensitivity to the three assumptions that move ROI most.

Do not select only winning comparables. Include failures, base rates and counter-evidence.

## Forward test

Every forward test must state before launch:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
What must be true?

TEST
What is the smallest real-market action?

METRIC
What behaviour or cash movement is measured?

THRESHOLD
What exact result means Go, Kill, Hold or Recycle?

BUDGET / TIMEBOX
What is the maximum money, founder time and elapsed time?
```

## Live pilot

A live pilot must record:

- actual cash collected;
- acquisition source and cost;
- actual sale-to-first-value time;
- direct delivery costs;
- human minutes;
- failures/refunds/support;
- customer outcome;
- contribution after CAC;
- what must change before the next customer.

# 7. Model-specific benchmark cards

Use the universal score above, then attach the relevant model card. Do not force SaaS benchmarks onto a royalty, marketplace, digital-product or service business.

## Digital product / Blueprint

Track:

- qualified traffic;
- checkout conversion;
- upfront price;
- payment/platform fees;
- refund rate;
- support minutes per sale;
- contribution per sale;
- completion/activation;
- repeat purchase/upsell;
- royalties and referral commissions;
- platform concentration.

## SaaS / subscription

Track:

- activation;
- MRR/ARR;
- gross and net revenue retention;
- logo/customer churn;
- gross margin;
- contribution margin;
- CAC and payback;
- LTV:CAC when retention history is sufficient;
- usage/adoption;
- support and compute cost;
- runway and growth efficiency.

## Marketplace

Track:

- GMV and net revenue;
- take rate;
- buyer/seller acquisition cost;
- liquidity/fill rate;
- time to first match/transaction;
- repeat ratio;
- fulfilment speed;
- disputes/refunds;
- contribution per transaction;
- supply and demand concentration.

## Tech-enabled service / agency

Track:

- revenue and gross profit per client;
- client retention and expansion;
- utilisation and delivery hours;
- founder hours;
- contribution per client;
- CAC and payback;
- quality/outcome rate;
- standardisation and capacity without new headcount.

## AI usage / outcome business

Track:

- cost per successful outcome;
- success/failure/retry rate;
- compute, model and provider cost;
- human recovery minutes;
- customer value per outcome;
- contribution after failed runs;
- usage retention;
- vendor/model dependency;
- deterministic fallback.

Traditional businesses require separate cards for location economics, inventory, labour, rent, working capital, utilisation, spoilage and local demand. Do not dilute the digital factory now; add those cards when a traditional business enters active research.

# 8. Department-as-business scorecard

Every department must own a measurable economic job. Do not invent fictional internal sales.

Classify the department as one or more of:

1. **Create revenue** — acquisition, sales, product, partnerships.
2. **Protect revenue** — customer success, quality, compliance, security.
3. **Improve margin** — operations, procurement, automation, delivery.
4. **Accelerate cash** — billing, collections, finance, working capital.
5. **Enable future value** — people, data, legal and technology; must link to one of the first four through a measurable leading indicator.

## Department scorecard

| Metric | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Annual department cost | Fully loaded cost |
| Economic job | Create, protect, improve, accelerate or enable |
| Output volume | Qualified outputs completed |
| Quality/success rate | Percentage meeting standard |
| Cycle time | Time from input to useful output/cash |
| Verified economic value | Gross profit created/protected, cost removed or cash released |
| Department ROI | `(verified economic value - department cost) ÷ department cost` |
| Capacity | Output before more people/capital are required |
| Dependency | Key person/vendor/system concentration |
| Next improvement test | One measurable change only |

Revenue attribution must use conservative incrementality. Do not claim all company revenue for marketing, sales, operations or product simultaneously.

# 9. Blueprint Certification

A Blueprint is the documented recipe for reproducing a business result. It is not merely a PDF, template or strategy description.

A P6 Blueprint package must contain:

- exact target buyer and use case;
- offer and all revenue streams;
- pricing and unit economics;
- startup capital and founder-time requirements;
- 12-month downside/base/upside model;
- acquisition process and evidence;
- delivery process and evidence;
- tools, inputs and dependencies;
- KPI dashboard;
- known failure modes and recovery;
- legal/platform boundaries;
- proof period, sample size and evidence register;
- operating checklist;
- update/version policy;
- clear statement of what results are not guaranteed.

## Certification labels

- **Experimental Blueprint — P3:** forward-tested, not yet revenue-proven.
- **Revenue-Proven Blueprint — P4:** at least one paid successful use.
- **Repeatable Blueprint — P5:** repeated across customers/cohorts.
- **Blueprint Certified — P6:** scale and reproducibility verified.

Only P6 may use the unqualified label **Blueprint Certified**.

# 10. Portfolio migration rule

The current DRF v1.5 Opportunity Scores remain useful historical structural screens, but they are **not investor-readiness ratings**.

For every existing opportunity:

1. preserve the existing dossier as evidence history;
2. start a blank current template;
3. rebuild the money model and revenue streams;
4. assign the correct model card;
5. reverify critical current evidence;
6. build the three-tier financial model;
7. conduct the backtest;
8. assign RBS, Proof Level, Investment Gate and Return Profile;
9. compare old and new only after the new underwriting is complete;
10. update the portfolio table and retain an audit trail.

Until rebuilt, display:

> **Legacy structural score — not investor-ready; Proof Level and Return Profile pending.**
