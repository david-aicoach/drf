# DRF Business × Niche Underwriting, Proof and Investment Readiness

**Status:** Canonical Layer 2 underwriting/proof guideline  
**Version:** 2.0  
**Date:** 31 August 2026  
**Original governing issue:** #60  
**Three-layer DRF architecture:** [77.1] #78  
**Priority:** Digital and service businesses first; add model cards for traditional businesses when needed.

## Purpose

DRF is David's Revenue Factory. Its job is to discover, research, compare, select, adapt, test, improve, scale and optionally package revenue-producing businesses.

This guideline operates **after Layer 1 business-opportunity selection and Layer 2 niche selection**. It underwrites the selected Business × Niche, separates market proof from DRF execution proof, controls capital and defines the next proof milestone.

It does not replace:

- Opportunity Score;
- MRR potential;
- AI Autonomy;
- Evidence Confidence;
- Research Completeness;
- External Market Proof;
- Niche Attractiveness Score.

## Complete decision stack

Every serious opportunity must preserve the full stack:

```text
LAYER 1 — BUSINESS ATTRACTIVENESS
Opportunity Score + MRR + AI Autonomy + Evidence Confidence
+ Research Completeness + External Market Proof

LAYER 2 — TARGET + COMMERCIAL UNDERWRITING
Best Niche + Niche Score + Niche Evidence Confidence
+ Offer + Pricing + GTM + Delivery Architecture
+ Revenue Blueprint Score + Return Profile

DRF EXECUTION CONTROL
DRF Proof P0–P6 + Stage + Capital + Next Proof

LAYER 3 — STRUCTURED OUTPUT
Comparable V3 portfolio row + founder-readable mini business plan
```

Never show RBS as if it were the entire DRF decision.

## Founder-facing decision line

Use this format wherever space allows:

> **Opportunity 95/100 · MRR 10/10 · AI Autonomy 95/100 · Best Niche: UAE HVAC 92/100 · External Market Proof: EMP3 Market Proven · RBS 86/100 · DRF Proof: P2 Backtested · Stage: TEST · Capital: up to $3,000 · Next Proof: two paid pilot deposits**

Compact surfaces may omit secondary detail, but must not present a standalone score as proof.

`Investor-ready` is not a required primary dashboard column. It may remain a derived detailed control where useful.

# 0. External Market Proof vs DRF Proof

These are separate dimensions.

## External Market Proof

External Market Proof asks:

> **Do materially similar businesses already operate successfully in the real market?**

The canonical EMP0–EMP4 levels and transferability rules are defined in:

`knowledge/guidelines/business-opportunity-scoring-framework.md`

External Market Proof can establish that:

- buyers already pay for the category/outcome;
- workable offers and pricing exist;
- recurring revenue, acquisition and delivery patterns are observable;
- multiple operators have built viable businesses;
- known failure modes and counter-evidence exist.

Strong EMP may support high Opportunity and Niche factor scores, accelerate desk underwriting and provide P2 backtest comparables.

It does **not** prove DRF's exact local offer, price, acquisition cost, delivery quality, unit economics or repeatability.

## DRF Proof

DRF Proof P0–P6 asks:

> **How far has DRF's selected adaptation progressed?**

A valid state is:

`External Market Proof: EMP3 Market Proven · DRF Proof: P1 Desk Underwritten`

Do not describe that business model as “unproven”. The **category is market proven**; DRF's adaptation is not yet operationally proven.

## Remaining-uncertainty rule

After studying successful operators, test only what remains materially uncertain for DRF:

- selected niche/geography transferability;
- exact offer and price;
- reachable acquisition channel and CAC;
- local sales cycle;
- delivery quality and direct costs;
- contribution margin;
- retention/recurring value;
- founder dependence;
- repeatability and scale.

Do not spend money re-proving broad facts already strongly established by current market evidence.

# 1. Revenue Blueprint Score — 0 to 100

The Revenue Blueprint Score (RBS) measures the deeper structural and economic quality of a **selected Business × Niche** after the offer, pricing, GTM, delivery and financial model are defined.

It is comparable across business models, but it is not a probability of success and it does not replace Opportunity Score or Niche Score.

Score every factor from 0 to 10 and multiply by its weight.

| Factor | Weight | Founder question |
|---|---:|---|
| **Demand, market and timing** | **15** | Are enough target buyers actively looking/spending now, and is timing favourable? |
| **Pain, willingness to pay and pricing power** | **10** | Is the problem expensive/urgent enough for the selected ICP to move money at the proposed price? |
| **Revenue quality and retention** | **10** | Is revenue recurring, expanding, repeat-purchased or otherwise durable? |
| **Unit economics and margin** | **15** | Does each sale/customer produce attractive gross and contribution profit after real costs? |
| **Customer acquisition and paid growth** | **10** | Can this niche be reached predictably with acceptable CAC and payback? |
| **Delivery repeatability and customer outcome** | **10** | Can the same offer create the promised result without bespoke reinvention? |
| **Scalability, leverage and founder independence** | **10** | Can revenue grow faster than labour, cost and founder time? |
| **Capital efficiency, payback and ROI** | **10** | How quickly and strongly can invested cash return? |
| **Durability, moat and concentration** | **5** | Can the business remain valuable despite competitors, vendor/platform changes and concentration? |
| **Operational, legal and platform risk** | **5** | How low and manageable are execution, compliance, dependency and downside risks? Higher is better. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

`weighted points = factor score ÷ 10 × factor weight`

`Revenue Blueprint Score = sum(weighted points)`

## RBS score bands

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **85–100** | Structurally exceptional Business × Niche |
| **75–84** | Structurally strong |
| **65–74** | Conditional/testable |
| **50–64** | Weak unless one material change improves the case |
| **Below 50** | Reject, park or recycle |

A score band controls **interest**, not capital.

## Scoring anchors

- **0–2:** evidence materially contradicts the case.
- **3–4:** weak, difficult or dependent on unsupported assumptions.
- **5–6:** plausible but ordinary, mixed or uncertain.
- **7–8:** strong evidence or structural advantage.
- **9–10:** exceptional advantage supported by strong evidence and few realistic substitutes/constraints.

## Evidence discipline

Every factor must link to:

- evidence used;
- date;
- evidence class;
- external/operator source where relevant;
- most important counter-evidence;
- remaining assumption.

A factor may score highly from strong external evidence while DRF remains P1. DRF Proof prevents market/category proof from being confused with our execution.

# 2. DRF Proof Level — P0 to P6

DRF Proof states how far **DRF's selected adaptation** has moved from capture to reproducible business.

| Level | Name | Minimum DRF evidence | What DRF may claim |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **P0** | **Captured** | Clear business opportunity, payer, outcome and revenue logic | Opportunity captured |
| **P1** | **Desk Underwritten** | Layer 1 scores, ranked niches, successful comparable operators, selected offer/price/GTM/delivery, initial RBS and financial model | DRF adaptation is structurally researched |
| **P2** | **Backtested** | External/comparable/historical evidence replayed through downside/base/upside; sensitivities, break-even, maximum loss and transferability documented | DRF economics survive a documented comparative stress test |
| **P3** | **Forward Tested** | Current real-market test of the remaining DRF-specific demand/price/channel uncertainty against a pre-written threshold | Current market has taken a meaningful commercial action |
| **P4** | **Revenue Proven** | Genuine payment plus delivery/activation, first-value evidence and actual cost capture | DRF sold and delivered value once |
| **P5** | **Repeatable** | Same core offer acquired/delivered across at least three independent customers/cohorts/cycles with positive contribution and bounded founder effort | A repeatable DRF small business exists |
| **P6** | **Scale Proven / Blueprint Certified** | Acquisition, delivery, margin, retention and capacity hold at meaningful volume; another competent operator can reproduce it | Scale-ready and packageable as an evidence-backed Blueprint |

## Proof rules

- Strong External Market Proof can support P1/P2; it cannot by itself award P3–P6.
- Interviews are not revenue. They can support P3 only when testing a defined commercial hypothesis.
- Letters of intent are weaker than deposits; deposits are weaker than completed paid delivery.
- One sale proves payment and first value, not repeatability.
- Three customers are a minimum pattern check, not statistical certainty; high-volume products require meaningful cohorts.
- Founder rescue of every delivery does not prove repeatability.
- Profitable organic acquisition does not prove paid growth.
- P6 must disclose evidence period, sample size, exclusions and known failure modes.

## Evidence classes

1. **E0 — Assumption:** founder/agent belief.
2. **E1 — Published/external evidence:** first-party, independent, operator or market data.
3. **E2 — Backtest evidence:** historical/comparable evidence tested against DRF's model.
4. **E3 — DRF forward-test evidence:** current behaviour before full delivery.
5. **E4 — DRF live operating evidence:** payments, delivery, outcomes and actual costs.
6. **E5 — DRF repeated/cohort evidence:** multiple customers/cycles with contribution and retention/repeat behaviour.
7. **E6 — DRF verified scale evidence:** stable operating results at volume, with reconciled records where practical.

Every important number must show its evidence class.

# 3. Stage and decision

The founder-facing dashboard label is **Stage**.

| Stage | Typical DRF Proof | Allowed action |
|---|---:|---|
| **REJECT** | Any | Stop; preserve evidence and reason |
| **RESEARCH** | P0–P1 | Remove one major uncertainty; desk work only |
| **TEST** | P1–P2 | Run the smallest current-market price/demand/channel test |
| **PILOT** | P3 | Complete bounded paid delivery |
| **FUND** | P4 | Build the repeatable small business |
| **SCALE** | P5 | Increase acquisition/capacity only when unit economics and capacity pass |
| **BLUEPRINT** | P6 | Package/distribute the proven operating system and continue measuring cohorts |

The underlying decision must be one of:

```text
GO · KILL · HOLD · RECYCLE
```

- **GO:** proceed to the next Stage/capital tranche.
- **KILL:** stop and preserve the lesson/evidence.
- **HOLD:** timing/dependency blocks action.
- **RECYCLE:** change one material offer, niche, price, channel or delivery assumption and re-underwrite.

Use `Investment Gate` only as a detailed/internal synonym where needed. The main founder table should display **Stage**.

# 4. Three default capital tiers

Capital buys evidence, not activity.

| Tier | New capital | Cumulative | Purpose | DRF Proof target | Unlock condition |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Validate** | **Up to $3,000** | **$3,000** | Smallest sellable offer, price/channel test and paid-pilot evidence | P3–P4 | P1 underwriting and P2 backtest pass |
| **Tier 2 — Launch** | **+$7,000** | **$10,000** | Acquire/serve enough customers to prove repeatability and positive contribution | P5 | At least one paid successful delivery; no fatal unit-economics issue |
| **Tier 3 — Scale** | **+$20,000** | **$30,000** | Scale proven channel, automate delivery, add capacity/working capital | P6 | P5 repeatability, CAC/payback, delivery capacity and downside survival pass |

These are default comparison caps, not spending targets. Prove the gate for less whenever possible.

## Use-of-funds rule

Every tier must state:

- exact use of funds;
- founder hours;
- evidence/milestone purchased;
- maximum loss;
- stop date;
- next capital unlock condition.

Do not release capital to “build the platform” when the unresolved risk is demand, price, niche or distribution.

# 5. Return Profile

Every selected Business × Niche must include downside, base and upside cases with a 12-month monthly forecast once Stage requires it.

## Required outputs

- startup/staged capital;
- revenue by stream;
- gross profit;
- contribution before/after CAC;
- monthly net cash flow;
- cumulative cash;
- break-even revenue/month;
- runway and maximum cash loss;
- CAC/payback;
- retention/repeat/churn;
- monthly cash-on-cash ROI;
- year-one ROI;
- founder hours/month;
- cash generated per founder hour;
- employment-replacement month against the chosen owner-income target.

## Core formulas

```text
Contribution per sale
= sale price
- refunds/discounts
- payment/platform fees
- variable fulfilment
- variable support/recovery cost

Contribution after CAC
= contribution per sale - CAC

Break-even sales per month
= fixed monthly costs ÷ contribution per sale before fixed costs

Break-even ROAS
= 1 ÷ contribution margin before advertising

Monthly cash-on-cash ROI
= monthly net cash flow ÷ cumulative capital invested

Year-one ROI
= total year-one net cash flow ÷ cumulative capital invested

Founder hourly return
= monthly net cash flow ÷ founder hours
```

Use cash rather than revenue for return calculations. Show the basis beside every return figure.

Unknowns must display **Pending/Missing**, never false zero.

## Funding-readiness detail

A business should not receive meaningful growth capital unless all relevant controls are present:

- P4 or higher for Tier 2;
- identified paying customer/revenue stream;
- delivered value/activation evidence;
- measured or tightly bounded contribution margin;
- 12-month downside/base/upside forecast;
- use of funds tied to next proof;
- maximum downside/runway;
- founder-time requirement;
- material legal/platform conditions verified/gated;
- one acquisition path with real evidence;
- no assumption presented as fact.

Scale capital requires P5.

# 6. Backtest, forward test and live proof

## Comparative/business backtest

A backtest replays DRF's proposed Business × Niche against the strongest available evidence:

- successful comparable operator pricing and economics;
- failed operators and counter-evidence;
- historical client/DRF/Talent Bridge/iMPLEMENTAi data;
- marketplace sales/review data;
- advertising demand/CPC/audience data;
- public company/category unit economics;
- analogous delivery/support costs.

Stress at least:

- demand/conversion;
- price/ARPU/take rate;
- CAC;
- churn/refunds;
- gross margin/provider/compute cost;
- founder/delivery hours;
- platform/commission/royalty rules;
- downside cash loss;
- the three assumptions moving ROI most.

External evidence may prove the category; the backtest must test whether the pattern transfers to DRF's selected niche, offer, channel and cost structure.

## Forward test

Write before launch:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
What DRF-specific uncertainty must be true?

TEST
What is the smallest current-market action?

METRIC
What behaviour or cash movement is measured?

PASS / FAIL / RECYCLE THRESHOLD
What exact result and date control the decision?

BUDGET / TIMEBOX
Maximum money, founder time and elapsed time
```

Do not run a generic “validation” test. Test the largest remaining uncertainty after external proof and backtesting.

## Live pilot

Record:

- actual cash collected;
- acquisition source/cost;
- buyer/use case;
- sale-to-first-value time;
- direct platform/payment/provider/AI costs;
- founder/team hours;
- failures, support, refunds and recovery;
- customer outcome;
- gross/contribution margin;
- what must change before customer two.

# 7. Model-specific benchmark cards

Use the universal RBS, then attach the relevant model card. Do not force SaaS benchmarks onto services, royalties, marketplaces or digital products.

## Digital product / Blueprint

Track qualified traffic, checkout conversion, price, fees, refunds, support minutes, contribution per sale, activation/completion, repeat/upsell, royalties/referrals and platform concentration.

## SaaS / subscription

Track activation, MRR/ARR, gross/net retention, churn, gross/contribution margin, CAC/payback, LTV:CAC when valid, usage, support/compute cost, runway and growth efficiency.

## Marketplace

Track GMV/net revenue, take rate, buyer/seller CAC, liquidity/fill rate, time to first match, repeat ratio, fulfilment, disputes/refunds, contribution/transaction and concentration.

## Tech-enabled service / agency

Track revenue/gross profit/client, retention/expansion, delivery hours, founder hours, contribution/client, CAC/payback, outcome rate, standardisation and capacity without proportional headcount.

## AI usage / outcome business

Track cost/successful outcome, success/failure/retry rate, compute/provider cost, human recovery minutes, customer value/outcome, contribution after failed runs, usage retention, vendor dependency and deterministic fallback.

Traditional businesses require separate cards for location economics, inventory, labour, rent, working capital, utilisation, spoilage and local demand when one enters active research.

# 8. Department-as-business scorecard

Every department must own a measurable economic job. Do not invent fictional internal sales.

Classify it as one or more of:

1. **Create revenue** — acquisition, sales, product, partnerships.
2. **Protect revenue** — customer success, quality, compliance, security.
3. **Improve margin** — operations, procurement, automation, delivery.
4. **Accelerate cash** — billing, collections, finance, working capital.
5. **Enable future value** — people, data, legal and technology linked to a measurable leading indicator.

| Metric | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Annual department cost | Fully loaded cost |
| Economic job | Create, protect, improve, accelerate or enable |
| Output volume | Qualified outputs completed |
| Quality/success rate | Percentage meeting standard |
| Cycle time | Input to useful output/cash |
| Verified economic value | Gross profit created/protected, cost removed or cash released |
| Department ROI | `(verified economic value - department cost) ÷ department cost` |
| Capacity | Output before more people/capital are required |
| Dependency | Key person/vendor/system concentration |
| Next improvement test | One measurable change |

Use conservative incrementality. Do not claim the same revenue for multiple departments.

# 9. Blueprint packaging and certification

A Business Blueprint is a documented recipe for reproducing a business result. It is not merely a PDF, template or strategy description.

Blueprint packaging is optional after sufficient understanding/proof. It is not the identity of every DRF opportunity.

A P6 Blueprint package must contain:

- exact target buyer/use case;
- offer and revenue streams;
- pricing and unit economics;
- startup capital/founder time;
- 12-month scenarios;
- acquisition process/evidence;
- delivery process/evidence;
- tools/inputs/dependencies;
- KPI dashboard;
- failure/recovery;
- legal/platform boundaries;
- proof period/sample/evidence register;
- operating checklist;
- update/version policy;
- no-guarantee statement.

## Packaging labels

- **Experimental Blueprint — P3**
- **Revenue-Proven Blueprint — P4**
- **Repeatable Blueprint — P5**
- **Blueprint Certified — P6**

Only P6 may use the unqualified label **Blueprint Certified**.

`Business Blueprints` may also be separately scored as a DRF business opportunity for selling packages through compatible channels. Do not conflate that opportunity with the optional packaging stage of other businesses.

# 10. Portfolio progression rule

Opportunity Score remains a first-class Layer 1 metric. RBS does not supersede it.

For every opportunity:

1. preserve Layer 1 structural research and scores;
2. preserve External Market Proof and counter-evidence;
3. rank niches and select the current beachhead;
4. define the Business × Niche offer, price, GTM and delivery architecture;
5. assign the correct model card;
6. build the staged financial model;
7. backtest transferability/economics;
8. assign RBS, DRF Proof, Stage, Capital and Next Proof;
9. produce/update the structured Layer 3 business case;
10. retain an evidence/version audit trail.

Until Layer 2 is complete, display:

> **Layer 1 structurally scored · Business × Niche underwriting, DRF Proof and Return Profile pending.**

Do not display missing fields as zero.
