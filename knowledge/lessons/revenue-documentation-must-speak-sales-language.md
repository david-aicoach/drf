# Lesson — Revenue documentation must speak sales language

**Date:** 31 August 2026  
**Origin:** Issue #50 / Whop Business Blueprints clarification  
**Applies to:** all DRF business, opportunity, offer, pricing, monetisation and commercial research

## Lesson

**DRF is the Revenue Factory. Commercial documentation must read like a business owner is trying to understand how money is made.**

Do not make the founder translate platform terminology, analyst language or technical abstractions into a business model.

The commercial summary must come first. Detail and evidence come after it.

## Founder-first commercial summary

For every revenue opportunity, answer these questions at the top of the document:

1. **What are we selling?**
2. **Who pays us?**
3. **How do we get paid?**
4. **How much, or what is the payment based on?**
5. **Is the income upfront, recurring, a royalty, a commission, usage revenue or another clearly named type?**
6. **Which revenue streams are core and which are optional upsells?**

If those six answers are not obvious within seconds, the commercial summary is not good enough.

## Use founder language

Prefer words that describe the actual money movement.

| Avoid leading with | Prefer |
|---|---|
| creator economics | royalty / creator royalty / business royalty |
| downstream economics | recurring royalty from businesses using the product |
| partner economics | referral commission |
| monetisation layer | revenue stream |
| implementation layer | optional setup/consulting upsell |
| creator share | royalty percentage / commission percentage |
| commercial mechanism | how we get paid |
| platform-funded economics | payment or commission paid by the platform |

Technical or programme terminology may still be used in the detailed evidence section when it is the official source wording. It must not replace the plain-English business explanation.

## Required structure

Use this order for commercial research:

```text
BUSINESS SUMMARY
→ what we sell
→ who pays
→ revenue streams
→ price / percentage / calculation basis
→ upfront vs recurring
→ core vs optional

THEN DETAIL
→ programme mechanics
→ evidence
→ assumptions
→ risks
→ market data
→ technical detail
→ validation plan
```

Do not force the reader to work through research before discovering the business model.

## Example — Whop Business Blueprints

The confusing version described creator economics, downstream Whop economics and implementation layers before clearly stating what the business earns.

The founder version is:

```text
1. BLUEPRINT SALES
Upfront cash from selling the packaged Blueprint/IP.

2. BLUEPRINT BUSINESS ROYALTIES
Recurring royalties when businesses deploy and operate using our Blueprint.

3. WHOP REFERRAL COMMISSIONS
Recurring referral income where Whop Partner attribution applies.

OPTIONAL UPSIDE
Implementation, consulting or managed services if we choose to sell them.
```

That immediately answers the business question.

## Evidence discipline still applies

Plain language does not mean loose claims.

If a number is not verified, say so beside the number. Example:

> **Blueprint business royalty:** reported as 10% of Whop gross profit attributable to businesses using the Blueprint; exact first-party payout wording still requires direct capture.

Do not hide uncertainty behind vague language, but do not make the whole commercial model vague merely because one percentage is still being verified.

## Agent test before finishing revenue research

Before committing a business or opportunity document, ask:

> **Could a founder understand in 20 seconds what is being sold, who pays, how the money arrives, and which income is upfront versus recurring?**

If not, rewrite the summary before adding more research.

## Why this matters

DRF exists to identify, prove and scale revenue. Confusing revenue language creates decision friction, hides the real commercial model and can cause incorrect assumptions about pricing, margins and recurring income.

**Sales clarity is part of commercial accuracy.**
