## Portfolio conclusion

Both opportunities survive research, but neither survives in exactly its original form.

### Business Blueprints — Whop-channel validation (historical)

The opportunity remains unusually attractive because the new Blueprint primitive is launching on top of an already scaled commerce network. Whop currently says sellers earned **$300M+ in the last month**, its marketplace has **22M+ monthly active users**, and it powers **27,000+ fast-growing businesses**. Its July 2026 CLI/MCP releases also make substantial business operation agent-accessible.

The score falls from 86 to 83 because the most important economics are still not first-party verified: public early-access reporting says a Blueprint creator receives **10% of Whop gross profit generated by onboarded businesses**, but no definitive public Whop terms page confirming that exact rate was located in this research pass. More importantly, no DRF Blueprint has yet generated a deployment, payout, retention history, support burden or measured CAC.

**Decision:** enough evidence exists to move from Researching to Candidate, but not to scale.

### AI-First Marketplace Directory

The underlying problem is strongly validated: digital discovery is fragmented and buyers are moving rapidly from conventional browsing into AI-mediated discovery. However, the original horizontal first-mover thesis is materially weakened.

G2 already serves more than **200M annual buyers**, lists **200,000+ products and services**, spans **2,000+ categories**, and launched MCP support in March 2026. Futurepedia monetises AI-tool listings at **$497** for verified placement. SaaSHub reports **564,000+ monthly visitors** and charges **$99/month** for featured listings. At the same time, OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol and Google's Universal Commerce Protocol are moving structured merchant discovery directly into AI interfaces.

The largest structural constraint is data rights. Upwork explicitly prohibits scraping and using its service to build a similar service; Fiverr explicitly prohibits systematic retrieval to create a collection, database or directory; Product Hunt says its API may not be used commercially without permission; Etsy requires approved Commercial Access and compliance with API/caching rules. Therefore the original idea of cheaply indexing broad supply from many marketplaces cannot be assumed viable.

**Decision:** the generic horizontal directory should not be built. The surviving opportunity is a narrower **licensed / merchant-submitted normalised supply graph + intent-routing layer** in a fragmented vertical that incumbents and retail commerce protocols do not already cover well.

## 1.2 What is now verified

### A. The underlying platform already has meaningful commerce

Whop's current network page states:

- sellers earned **$300M+ on Whop last month**;
- its marketplace has **22M+ MAU**;
- Whop powers **27,000+** fast-growing businesses;
- listing in the marketplace is free.

Source: https://whop.com/network/

This matters because Blueprints are not attempting to create demand on an empty marketplace. The new product is being inserted into an existing transaction and distribution layer.

### B. Whop has built an unusually agent-operable business stack

Whop's 21 July 2026 CLI launch says its end-to-end API can expose dashboard operations through terminal commands and agents. Whop explicitly supports `whop mcp add` and `whop skills add`; products, pricing, checkout, ads, analytics and other business operations can be driven programmatically.

Whop's 27 July guide says Claude Code, Cursor and Codex can run business workflows via the CLI, including reporting, catalogue management, ads and operational monitoring.

Sources:

- https://whop.com/blog/cli/
- https://whop.com/blog/run-business-with-cli/
- https://newsroom.whop.com/cli/

This strongly validates AI Buildability and AI Marketability, and materially supports AI Deliverability.

### C. Business Blueprints are genuinely at category-formation stage

Whop's public announcement on 29 August 2026 describes two sides of the new product:

- select a Blueprint and go from zero to a business;
- build a Blueprint and get paid when other businesses use it.

Primary announcement supplied by David:

https://x.com/whop/status/2093042100707135961?s=46

Current public repost/archive evidence:

- https://zamantika.com/siyabuilt/status/2093042449161589091

The timing advantage is therefore real: Whop itself is mature enough to provide demand and infrastructure, while the Blueprint category is effectively new.

### D. The apparent product is more than a document/template

An early-access builder reports that Blueprints can reproduce the practical business setup including site, products, checkout, payments, analytics and advertising pixel configuration.

Source:

- https://zamantika.com/siyabuilt/status/2093042449161589091

This is not first-party programme documentation, so individual capability claims should be verified in the live builder before being treated as contractual product scope.

### E. Platform economics support meaningful end-customer spending

Whop currently prices domestic card processing from **2.7% + $0.30** per successful transaction, with additional fees for international cards, FX and optional services.

Source:

- https://whop.com/network/pricing/

Independent marketplace analysis also shows strong power-law economics rather than uniform seller success. Whop Trends' February 2026 dataset tracked 191,654 products, estimated 11.2% were earning revenue, and estimated only 2% exceeded $1,000/month. These are third-party estimates, not Whop financial statements, but they are a useful counterweight to platform-level growth claims.

Source:

- https://whoptrends.com/blog/whop-creator-earnings-data-2026

**Implication:** Whop scale is validated; automatic seller success is not.

## 1.3 What remains unverified

### Blueprint creator payout formula

Early-access community reporting says a Blueprint creator earns **10% of Whop's gross profit** from each business onboarded through the Blueprint, recurring while the business operates.

Sources:

- https://zamantika.com/siyabuilt/status/2093042449161589091
- https://mobile.twstalker.com/eptwts

This is strategically important but remains **Estimated / Unverified** until it is visible in first-party Blueprint programme terms, the live creator interface or direct written confirmation from Whop.

Do not forecast revenue using 10% as a guaranteed contractual rate.

### Real Blueprint conversion and retention

Still missing:

- percentage of visitors who deploy a Blueprint;
- percentage of deployed businesses that generate revenue;
- time from deployment to first downstream sale;
- average downstream GMV / gross profit;
- Blueprint-derived creator payout;
- churn / survival rate of deployed businesses;
- support and exception burden;
- organic Whop discovery performance for a Blueprint;
- paid CAC / ROAS for acquiring Blueprint operators.

These are the facts the first live DRF test must obtain.

## 1.4 Human dependency and operating risk

Whop's current Seller Terms make clear that sellers remain responsible for product description, pricing, delivery, access, customer support, returns/refunds, compliance and general business operations. Sellers are also responsible for chargebacks and must maintain dispute rates within Whop/payment-network thresholds.

Source:

- https://whop.com/seller-terms

This prevents a simplistic conclusion that "Blueprint + AI = zero-human business". The Blueprint creator may automate much of configuration and operation, but the downstream operator still owns real commercial obligations. A viable Blueprint therefore needs to minimise support complexity rather than merely maximise features.

## 1.6 Why the score fell despite stronger evidence

Research confidence increased, but the commercial score fell from 86 to 83 because earlier scoring gave too much credit to unproven Blueprint-specific willingness-to-pay, delivery autonomy, margins and paid growth.

This is a healthy correction: **better evidence can increase confidence while lowering the attractiveness score.**

## 1.7 Bounded validation test

Do not create a catalogue of Blueprints yet.

Run one test:

```text
one narrow business model DRF genuinely understands
→ verify official Blueprint creator economics in live terms/UI
→ build one deployable Blueprint
→ create one real demonstration business
→ distribute through Whop + owned niche content + direct qualified outreach
→ observe deployments and downstream business activity
→ record support time, activation, revenue and payout economics
→ rescore
```

### Recommended first wedge

Prefer a vertical where the operating journey is already understood and digitally deliverable, rather than a generic "AI automation" Blueprint. Candidates include a productised agency or recruitment/staffing operating setup, but the actual first Blueprint should be selected from a model with a provable offer, customer journey and low support complexity.

### Test metrics

Record at minimum:

- qualified Blueprint views/demos;
- deployment/start rate;
- activation rate;
- first downstream transaction time;
- downstream GMV;
- actual Blueprint creator payout formula and amount;
- support minutes per deployed business;
- refund/dispute incidents;
- 30-day active-business retention;
- acquisition source and CAC where paid spend is used.

### Experimental stop condition

Pause rather than scale if any of the following occurs:

- official creator economics are materially worse than the working hypothesis;
- deployment requires significant recurring human setup/support;
- qualified prospects consistently prefer direct setup/service rather than the Blueprint;
- deployments fail to activate into real downstream commerce;
- compliance/refund/dispute burden destroys the expected margin.

# 2. AI-First Marketplace Directory

## 2.1 Validation verdict

**Stage: Researching**  
**Opportunity Score: 76.0 → 76/100**  
**AI Autonomy: 88/100**  
**Evidence Confidence: 72%**  
**Research Completeness: 86%**

The **problem is validated**. The **original broad horizontal implementation is not**.

The surviving opportunity is a narrow, rights-safe, structured discovery and routing business built around a specific fragmented supply class.

## 2.2 Demand for discovery is real

### G2 demonstrates very large commercial discovery demand

G2's current company page states that it serves more than **200M annual buyers**, with **200,000+ products and services**, **10,000+ customers** and **2,000+ categories**.

Source:

- https://company.g2.com/about

On 27 August 2026 G2 reported 30,274 products across 111 AI categories, with AI product listings up 630% YoY.

Source:

- https://company.g2.com/news/how-g2-is-bringing-accountability-to-ai-software-claims-in-2026

This validates the scale of structured B2B product discovery while simultaneously proving that G2 is a formidable incumbent.

### AI-mediated discovery is replacing part of conventional search

G2's July 2026 Buyer Behavior research, based on more than 1,000 B2B software buyers plus 50+ interviews, says the discovery phase has compressed from hours of browsing to a single prompt in an AI chatbot.

Source:

- https://company.g2.com/news/buyer-behavior-2026

OpenAI's March 2026 product-discovery launch similarly says more people are starting shopping in ChatGPT and extends the Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) so merchants can provide structured product feeds and promotions.

Source:

- https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/

Google announced the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) in January 2026 with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart, designed to interoperate with protocols including MCP.

Source:

- https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/nrf-2026-remarks/

**Implication:** agentic discovery is not a speculative trend. It is already becoming infrastructure.

## 2.3 Monetisation is validated — for directories with traffic/authority

### Futurepedia

Futurepedia says it reaches more than **400,000 proactive professionals per month** and currently prices its Verified Listing at **$497 one time**; enterprise packages are custom priced.

Source:

- https://www.futurepedia.io/submit-tool

### SaaSHub

SaaSHub says more than **564,000 people** visit monthly to compare products and alternatives. Its featured listing currently costs **$99/month** and it estimates 10–18 targeted referrals per month for featured products, while explicitly warning actual results vary.

Source:

- https://www.saashub.com/featured-products

These examples validate seller willingness to pay for discovery **after a directory has earned buyer attention**. They do not prove that a new entrant can cheaply acquire that attention.

## 2.4 The broad competitive gap is smaller than the original thesis assumed

### G2 is already becoming agent-native

G2 launched MCP support in March 2026 specifically to bring its structured trust/review data into AI workflows.

Source:

- https://company.g2.com/news/g2-launches-innovations-to-help-software-vendors-establish-trust-and-scale-in-the-ai-answer-economy

G2 therefore directly competes with the idea of becoming an AI-readable authority layer for B2B software and services.

### AI-tool directories are already crowded

Futurepedia, Toolify, There's An AI For That, SaaSHub and many smaller AI directories already compete for SEO, paid listings and recommendation authority. The generic "AI tools directory" wedge is therefore unattractive unless a new entrant owns proprietary data or distribution.

The opportunity must avoid being another list of links.

## 2.5 Data rights are the main structural constraint

The original concept contemplated indexing offers across many marketplaces. Current terms show that this cannot be treated as a free scraping problem.

### Upwork

Upwork's current Terms of Use prohibit robots/spiders/scrapers without written permission, prohibit copying/using information found on Upwork without consent, and prohibit using the service to build a similar service. Its API terms also restrict competitive use and aggregation.

Source:

- https://www.upwork.com/legal

### Fiverr

Fiverr's current terms prohibit automated scraping and explicitly prohibit systematically retrieving data/content to create or compile a collection, database or directory.

Source:

- https://www.fiverr.com/legal-portal/legal-terms/terms-of-service

### Product Hunt

Product Hunt's API documentation says public API access is read-only by default and the API **must not be used for commercial purposes** unless Product Hunt is contacted and permission is obtained.

Source:

- https://api.producthunt.com/v2/docs

### Etsy

Etsy provides Commercial Access to approved applications after manual review and requires compliance with its API and caching policies. This is a rights-based integration path, not permission to scrape indiscriminately.

Source:

- https://developer.etsy.com/documentation/

### Consequence

The MVP must ingest data only from:

- authorised APIs/feeds;
- commercial/affiliate programmes that permit the intended use;
- merchant/provider submissions;
- public/open data with clear reuse rights;
- explicit bilateral licences/partnerships.

**Scraping prohibited marketplaces is not a viable DRF business model.**

## 2.6 AI protocols are both tailwind and threat

### Tailwind

Structured discovery is becoming more valuable because AI systems need fresh, machine-readable product/provider data. Chrome's WebMCP origin trial in Chrome 149 lets websites expose structured tools directly to agents.

Source:

- https://developer.chrome.com/blog/ai-webmcp-origin-trial

### Threat

OpenAI ACP and Google UCP reduce the need for an independent horizontal consumer-product directory because merchants can increasingly push structured catalogues directly into the AI surfaces where users ask questions.

Therefore the directory should not compete head-on with the retail product graph.

## 2.7 Refined opportunity thesis

The strongest surviving version is:

> **A narrow, rights-safe normalised supply graph for fragmented digital B2B offers that cross the boundaries between product, template, software, agent, implementation service and ready-to-deploy business system — distributed to both humans and AI agents.**

Potential first wedge:

**Ready-to-deploy digital business systems** — e.g. business Blueprints, operating-system templates, niche websites, automation packages, specialised agents and implementation services.

Why this is better than the horizontal thesis:

- less direct overlap with G2's core software-review graph;
- less overlap with retail ACP/UCP catalogues;
- offers are economically valuable but structurally fragmented;
- DRF itself produces and evaluates this type of supply;
- product + service + implementation boundaries create useful normalisation work;
- merchant/provider submissions can become a lawful supply-acquisition channel;
- conversion/routing data can become proprietary over time.

The long-term moat would be **normalised data + freshness + permissioned supply + intent mapping + conversion evidence**, not a front-end directory.

## 2.9 Why the score fell while confidence rose sharply

The initial 81/100 assumed a 9/10 first-mover window, 10/10 market-size read, stronger paid growth and stronger moat.

Further research validated the market but invalidated those assumptions:

- major incumbents are already agent-enabling their data;
- OpenAI/Google are building direct commerce discovery protocols;
- many directory niches are crowded;
- data rights materially constrain broad ingestion;
- a new directory does not obtain seller willingness-to-pay until it proves traffic/conversion.

Evidence Confidence rises from 35% to 72% because we now know much more about the opportunity — including why the broad version is weaker.

## 2.10 Bounded validation test

Do not build a horizontal marketplace search engine.

Test one vertical:

```text
choose one fragmented high-value digital supply class
→ identify 5–10 lawful/permissioned supply sources
→ acquire 100–300 offers through API/feed/submission/manual rights-safe collection
→ normalise into one simple schema
→ publish comparison/intent pages
→ expose one machine-readable search interface
→ recruit providers to claim/update listings
→ measure buyer/agent usage and outbound commercial actions
→ attempt one monetisation mechanism
→ rescore
```

### Required test metrics

- indexed offers with confirmed reuse rights;
- percentage still fresh after 30 days;
- non-branded organic impressions/clicks;
- AI/referral traffic;
- agent/API searches;
- search-to-outbound-click rate;
- provider claim/update rate;
- affiliate/lead/listing revenue;
- revenue per 1,000 qualified visits/queries;
- ongoing data/freshness cost;
- support/partner minutes per provider.

### Experimental stop condition

Pause if:

- no narrow vertical can secure enough permissioned supply to be useful;
- freshness cost scales roughly linearly with listings without automation/partner updates;
- general AI search/G2/incumbents consistently satisfy the same intent better;
- providers will not claim/update listings or pay after meaningful qualified traffic is proven;
- outbound buyer intent does not convert into measurable commercial value.
