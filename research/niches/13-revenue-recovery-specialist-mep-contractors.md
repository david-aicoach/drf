## 1. Executive conclusion

Specialist MEP contractors remain attractive for revenue recovery because project/service quotations can be large, repeated and document-heavy, while the market is expanding. Mordor estimates the UAE facilities-management market at **US$23.59B in 2026**; hard services represented about **60.92%** of 2025 revenue, directly relevant to MEP/fire/asset work. Government procurement surfaces such as Dubai eSupply and DEWA show repeated formal tender/RFQ demand.

The deeper pass lowers the niche slightly from 88 to **87** because capable ERP/CRM/preconstruction platforms already cover quotation pipelines, reminders and tender management. Procore, Odoo and similar systems demonstrate that workflow software itself is not scarce.

The defensible offer is:

> **find technically valid quotations/opportunities that have gone commercially stale, restore a clear next action and measure recovered gross profit without changing engineering scope, price or contractual truth.**

## 2. Atomic ICP

Include UAE specialist HVAC, electrical, plumbing, fire/life-safety and fit-out MEP contractors that:

- issue repeated service/project quotations;
- have a sales/estimation team separate from delivery;
- hold open/stale opportunities in ERP, email, spreadsheets or CRM;
- perform AMC/service as well as project work where possible;
- have enough quote volume for monthly recovery to recur;
- can export quote number, customer, value, status and date.

Exclude mega-contractors with enterprise revenue-operations teams, tiny contractors with only a few quotes/month, and businesses with no reliable pricing/status data.

## 4. Growth/timing

- building stock and construction sustain hard-service demand;
- outsourced FM remains dominant;
- recurring AMC/service creates repeated quote/renewal cycles;
- contractors face margin pressure, increasing value of converting existing pipeline rather than buying more leads.

Software maturity is the counterforce. The timing window is managed recovery and revenue control around existing systems.

## 5. Buyer economics / WTP

MEP quotation values vary from small service work to six/seven-figure project packages. Therefore use client data, not an industry-wide average.

Recovery value formula:

`stale technically-valid quote value × realistic close probability × gross margin × avoidable no-follow-up share`

Example only: AED1M stale qualified quotes × 10% recoverable close probability × 20% gross margin = AED20k gross-profit opportunity. This is scenario maths, not a typical result.

WTP can be high when recovery is measured against gross profit and requires no new lead spend.

## 6. Workflow / failure points

```text
RFQ / client enquiry / site requirement
→ estimator / technical clarification
→ BOQ/scope/pricing
→ approval
→ quote submitted
→ technical/commercial questions
→ revisions
→ negotiation
→ award/loss/no decision
→ project/service delivery
```

Leakage:

- quote leaves estimator by email with no CRM stage;
- no owner after submission;
- follow-up depends on salesperson memory;
- revised quote replaces prior file without commercial history;
- “no response” remains indefinitely;
- technical blocker and commercial silence are not distinguished;
- lost reason is unknown;
- maintenance/project opportunities are mixed.

## 7. Quantified pain model

Create a quote ageing waterfall:

- 0–7 days;
- 8–30;
- 31–60;
- 61–90;
- 90+.

For each cohort track quote count/value, next-action presence, last customer interaction, expected margin, outcome and reason.

The first audit should answer: **what percentage of open quote value has no dated next action?**

## 8. Competitive analysis

### Incumbent platforms

- **Odoo** — CRM, quotations, accounting, project/field-service ecosystem; can automate reminders and preserve commercial data.
- **Procore** — tender/preconstruction platform centralising documents, invitations, bid comparison/levelling, estimating and downstream contracts; AI is increasingly embedded.
- other construction ERPs/estimating tools and generic CRMs already manage opportunity stages.

### Direct recovery competitors

- sales-automation/CRM agencies;
- outsourced telesales/BD teams;
- generic lead-nurture platforms.

### Substitute

- estimator/salesperson Outlook follow-ups;
- Excel quote log;
- weekly management meeting;
- ERP ageing report with manual chasing.

Competitive implication: the “recovery engine” must plug into system truth, not create a parallel quotation database. Competition Gap reduced to **7/10**.

## 9. Underserved gap

Most systems can store a quote. The gap is **commercial next-action discipline linked to recoverable value**.

DRF wedge:

- identify stale but valid quotes;
- classify technical vs commercial blockers;
- prepare context-aware follow-up;
- escalate exceptions;
- report recovered contribution.

## 10. Offer design

**Promise:** every valid open quotation has an owner, reason/status and dated next action; stale commercial opportunities receive disciplined follow-up until won, lost or disqualified.

Deliverables:

- quote ageing audit;
- recovery eligibility rules;
- 30/60/90+ day queues;
- email/WhatsApp follow-up sequences where permitted;
- human approval for material technical/pricing communications;
- recovered gross-profit dashboard.

## 11. Delivery architecture

```text
ERP/estimating/CRM = quotation truth
→ export/API/webhook
→ recovery eligibility/rules
→ CRM/workflow deterministic follow-up
→ optional agent prepares context for complex cases
→ human approves technical/contractual exceptions
→ outcome written back to source
```

Use Grok/computer-use only for cross-system research or document-heavy exceptions; repetitive reminders should be native automation.

## 12. Onboarding/friction

Need 90–180 days of quote history, gross-margin bands, customer ownership, ERP/CRM access, communication policies and technical escalation rules.

Friction: fragmented quote IDs, revisions, disputed scope, long procurement cycles, multiple decision makers, retention/consent rules and salesperson resistance.

## 13. Unit economics

Variable messaging cost is small relative to project values; human exception handling is the main cost. Gross margin improves when eligibility and escalation are standardised.

Target product model: automated queue + bounded human/agent context preparation, not outsourced full-time sales chasing.

## 14. Pricing hypothesis

- setup/audit: **AED5k–15k**;
- managed recovery: **AED3k–8k/month** depending quote volume/complexity;
- optional success fee on independently verifiable recovered gross profit, not merely invoiced revenue.

Validate contract/legal implications before performance-based pricing.

## 23. Live validation

Interview 15 contractors across HVAC/electrical/plumbing/fire/fit-out, obtain quote ageing from at least 5, then run one 60-day recovery pilot.

Measure quote value, next-action %, age, response, recovered wins, GP, communications/customer, human minutes and software cost.

**Pass:** ≥20% of qualified open quote value lacks next action, recovery produces ≥3× service fee in attributable GP, and support remains productisable.  
**Stop/narrow:** incumbent process already achieves high next-action compliance, technical complexity makes automation unsafe, or recoverable pool is too small.
