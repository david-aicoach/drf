# DRF Niche Research Standard v3

**Status:** Governing standard for comprehensive niche dossiers  
**Date:** 29 August 2026  
**Governing issue:** #46

A niche dossier is not a source list or summary note. It must support an investment/market-entry decision and explain both **how attractive the niche is** and **how DRF could reach and become discoverable to that niche**.

## Required sections

1. **Executive conclusion and current decision.** State the niche score, confidence, decision and the narrow reason to pursue, narrow or reject it.
2. **Atomic ICP, exclusions and trigger conditions.** Define geography, firmographic range, workflow characteristics, buyer and disqualifiers.
3. **Market definition, size, density and reachable prospect universe.** Prefer practical prospect counts to inflated TAM figures.
4. **Growth and timing/tailwind analysis.** Separate structural demand from temporary hype.
5. **Buyer economics and willingness-to-pay proxies.** Use job/treatment/order/contract values, gross-profit logic or avoided labour where evidence exists.
6. **Current customer journey / workflow map and failure points.** Show where revenue, time or service quality is lost.
7. **Quantified pain / revenue-leak model.** Use transparent scenario maths and label every non-observed input.
8. **Competitive analysis.** Separate:
   - direct competitors selling the same outcome;
   - vertical incumbent software/platforms;
   - agencies/consultants/managed-service providers;
   - manual/substitute methods such as WhatsApp Business, spreadsheets, staff memory or generic automation.
   Include public pricing and positioning where reliably available. A strong incumbent can lower the niche score even when confidence rises.
9. **Underserved-gap analysis and defensible wedge.** Explain exactly what remains unsolved and why DRF can solve it without duplicating mature software.
10. **Offer design and measurable customer outcome.** Lead with revenue, conversion, cash collection, response, booking, cost or operational reliability—not AI novelty.
11. **Delivery architecture and system-of-record boundaries.** Use the smallest reliable stack. Preserve capable incumbent CRM/PMS/DMS/ERP/FSM systems where appropriate.
12. **Implementation/onboarding requirements and likely friction.** Include data access, account ownership, consent, integration and user-adoption issues.
13. **Unit economics and gross-margin sensitivity.** Separate fixed platform costs, variable message/voice/inference costs and human support.
14. **Pricing hypothesis and commercial model.** State setup/implementation, recurring fee, usage allowance or performance component only where justified.
15. **Acquisition strategy and decision-maker map.** Define how the first 20–100 qualified prospects would actually be reached.
16. **SEO opportunity and competition.** Include:
   - transactional/money keyword themes;
   - informational/problem-aware keyword themes;
   - local/location intent where applicable;
   - likely SERP surface types: local pack/maps, directories, marketplaces, editorial lists, vendor pages, forums, video, government/regulator sources;
   - named current organic competitors found in research;
   - content/positioning gaps;
   - recommended primary money page;
   - supporting content cluster and internal-link logic;
   - local SEO requirements such as Google Business Profile, reviews and location/service pages where relevant;
   - search-volume or CPC numbers only when a credible current source is available. Never invent keyword volumes.
17. **AI discovery / GEO / answer-engine strategy.** Explain how the offer could earn citations or recommendations in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and other AI/search assistants. Cover:
   - allow legitimate search crawlers and keep important pages indexable;
   - explicitly allow **OAI-SearchBot** when ChatGPT Search discovery is desired;
   - submit and refresh discoverable URLs through normal search-engine tooling; use IndexNow where supported;
   - consistent organisation/service/location entity facts across the site, Google Business Profile and trustworthy directories;
   - accurate structured data that describes the real entity/service, without assuming schema guarantees ranking;
   - concise sourceable pages containing definitions, pricing ranges, methodology, comparisons, FAQs, regulatory facts and original data;
   - first-party case studies and benchmark data with dates/methodology;
   - third-party reviews, citations, memberships, directories and reputable media/industry mentions;
   - comparison/use-case pages that directly answer prompts buyers ask AI systems;
   - monitoring a fixed prompt set across major answer engines and recording citation/share-of-answer changes;
   - no fabricated reviews, fake citations, doorway sites or guaranteed-AI-ranking claims.
18. **Objections, switching barriers, compliance and operational risks.** Include sector-specific regulation and data/privacy constraints.
19. **Retention and expansion logic.** State why the monthly service continues to be valuable and which adjacent modules are earned rather than forced.
20. **Full 10-factor niche scoring rationale.** Score Pain/Urgency, Purchasing Power, Targetability, Growth, Volume, Competition Gap, ROI, Product-System Fit, Recurring Pain and Sales/Fulfilment Simplicity using the canonical framework.
21. **Evidence classification.** Separate **Verified**, **Estimated** and **Missing** claims. Do not let macro market evidence substitute for workflow proof.
22. **Source ledger.** Record source name, publisher, date where available, direct URL and what claim it supports. Prefer current first-party/government/regulatory evidence.
23. **Live-validation plan.** Specify sample size, data requested, workflow benchmark, metrics, pass/fail thresholds and stop conditions.

## Research quality hierarchy

1. UAE/Dubai government, regulators and official datasets.
2. Vendor/industry first-party documentation for capability, pricing and product facts.
3. High-quality current market research and audited/company-reported data.
4. Credible operator/competitor evidence and current SERP observations.
5. Broad/global benchmarks only where local evidence is unavailable, with transferability limits stated.

## SEO and AI-discovery evidence discipline

- Treat a search result page as a **competitive surface**, not a demand-size statistic.
- A high number of ranking vendors/directories means reachability but may also mean high competition.
- Do not infer exact monthly search volume from result counts.
- AI recommendation cannot be guaranteed or directly bought through ordinary SEO. The controllable inputs are crawl/index access, entity clarity, factual/sourceable content, third-party authority, reviews, original evidence and consistent monitoring.
- For local niches, Google Maps/Business Profile and strong third-party local citations can matter as much as the main website.
- For regulated/high-trust niches, authoritative regulator, professional and licensed-entity evidence should be built into both SEO and AI-answer content.

## Portfolio discipline

- A deeper pass can **lower a niche score while increasing Evidence Confidence**.
- Preserve mature incumbent systems and sell the measurable unsolved gap around them.
- Lead with measurable business outcomes, not software or model names.
- A comprehensive file must let another operator understand the score and first validation experiment without opening the parent register.
