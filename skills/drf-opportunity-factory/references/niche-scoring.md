# DRF Niche Attractiveness Scoring Framework

**Status:** Canonical Layer 2 market-selection guideline  
**Version:** 2.0  
**Date:** 31 August 2026  
**Original governing issue:** #26  
**Outcome-first architecture revision issue:** #33  
**Three-layer DRF architecture:** [77.1] #78

## Purpose

The DRF Business Opportunity Score answers:

> **What business/service/outcome is structurally attractive?**

This framework answers the next question:

> **Exactly which market should that outcome be sold to first?**

The niche layer strengthens the business opportunity; it does not replace it.

```text
Business Opportunity
→ Opportunity Score + MRR + AI Autonomy + Evidence + Research + External Market Proof
→ ranked Business × Niche options
→ Niche Attractiveness Score
→ selected Business × Niche
→ offer + pricing + GTM + delivery + RBS + DRF Proof + capital + return
```

Never treat a generic capability or vendor such as Revenue Recovery, Instant Quote, AI Voice, HighLevel, Kapso or Grok Bot as a complete go-to-market decision.

The atomic niche-selection unit is:

`outcome/product × vertical × sub-niche × geography × ICP × trigger/problem`

Example:

`Instant Quote × specialist construction × drywall/gypsum/false-ceiling installers × UAE × owner-led contractors receiving frequent quotation enquiries × slow/manual quoting`

After selecting the niche, define the full commercial deployment unit:

`Outcome × Niche × Customer Channel × System of Record × Agent Layer`

The Niche Score remains separate from Opportunity Score, delivery architecture and Revenue Blueprint Score. A great business can have a poor niche; a great niche can be a poor fit for one product; a strong Business × Niche can still fail if the offer, acquisition or delivery architecture is wrong.

---

# 1. Market-selection core

Use four fundamental market characteristics as the first gate:

1. **Massive pain** — an important, expensive, frequent or urgent problem.
2. **Purchasing power** — the prospect can economically pay for the outcome.
3. **Easy to target** — the decision-maker can be identified and reached efficiently.
4. **Growing/durable market** — expanding demand or a strong structural tailwind.

DRF extends these with factors required for productised services, vertical SaaS, outcome-based recurring products and AI-enabled delivery.

---

# 2. Niche Attractiveness Score — 0 to 100

Score each factor 0–10. Weights sum to 100.

| Factor | Weight | Research question |
|---|---:|---|
| **Pain / Urgency** | **15** | Is the problem acute, expensive, frequent and important enough to cause action now? |
| **Purchasing Power / Customer Economics** | **12** | Can the ICP comfortably pay the target setup/monthly price from normal business economics? |
| **Easy to Target / Reachability** | **10** | Can we build a precise prospect list and reach the decision-maker through ads, search, outbound, associations, directories or relationships? |
| **Market Growth / Tailwind** | **8** | Is the niche growing or benefiting from a durable structural trend? |
| **Market Volume / Density** | **10** | Are there enough qualified prospects in the geography to support the target without broadening the ICP? |
| **Underserved / Competition Gap** | **10** | Is specialised competition weak, generic or poorly served? Higher = stronger opportunity to differentiate. |
| **Measurable Revenue Upside / ROI** | **12** | Can the outcome be tied to recovered revenue, bookings, faster quotes, lower leakage or another measurable economic result? |
| **Product-System Fit** | **10** | Does the outcome solve the niche's real workflow with little bespoke engineering and a practical channel/system architecture? |
| **Recurring Pain / Retention Logic** | **8** | Does the pain recur so cancellation recreates meaningful leakage or cost? |
| **Sales + Fulfilment Simplicity** | **5** | Can the offer be explained, sold, onboarded and delivered repeatedly with low custom work and manageable compliance/integration resistance? |
| **Total** | **100** | |

`Niche Attractiveness Score = Σ((factor score / 10) × weight)`

## Decision bands

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| **85–100** | Sniper niche — priority launch/validation candidate |
| **75–84** | Strong niche — validate quickly |
| **65–74** | Testable — sharpen ICP or evidence |
| **50–64** | Weak/conditional — do not prioritise without a strategic reason |
| **Below 50** | Reject or materially redefine |

---

# 3. Niche Evidence Confidence — independent 0–100%

Every Niche Score must carry Niche Evidence Confidence.

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **80–100%** | Strong current niche-specific evidence, ideally including customers/operators/prospects |
| **60–79%** | Adequate for bounded underwriting/test |
| **40–59%** | Material assumptions remain; research first |
| **Below 40%** | Hypothesis only |

A niche scoring 90/100 with 35% confidence is **Research First**, not a launch priority.

Use the evidence labels defined in the Layer 1 framework: Verified fact, Credible estimate, Inference, DRF actual and Missing.

---

# 4. Proven-operator and External Market Proof evidence

Successful comparable operators are a required evidence source where they exist.

For each serious Business × Niche candidate, research:

- materially similar operators in the same or comparable niche/geography;
- their exact offer and positioning;
- setup, recurring, usage, licence, commission, royalty and upsell pricing;
- visible customer count, reviews, case studies, sales indicators, hiring, longevity or expansion;
- active advertising, funnels, SEO, outbound, partnerships and marketplaces;
- delivery model, onboarding, support and time to value;
- recurring value/retention logic;
- failed operators, weak reviews, complaints, churn, margin pressure and other counter-evidence.

## Category proof vs niche proof

Do not treat broad category proof as automatic proof of every niche.

Examples:

- CRM agencies succeeding generally support the category, but do not prove UAE HVAC contractors are the best beachhead for Enquiry-to-Revenue Control.
- AI voice companies succeeding generally support the business model, but do not prove every clinic or contractor has enough call leakage and booking value.
- Quote software succeeding generally supports willingness to pay, but drywall/gypsum fit depends on parameterisation, survey gates, ticket values and local workflow.

Record:

- **Business-level External Market Proof** — from the canonical Layer 1 framework;
- **Niche-specific comparable proof** — evidence that the model works for materially similar customers/pain;
- **Niche Evidence Confidence** — trust in the current score.

## Transferability questions

1. Is the payer role similar?
2. Is the purchase trigger similar?
3. Is the pain/value comparable?
4. Does the pricing transfer to this geography and ICP?
5. Can DRF reach the market through a comparable channel?
6. Are CRM/system, privacy, regulation or integration conditions different?
7. Does the incumbent's success rely on a unique brand, audience, dataset or founder?
8. What must DRF still prove locally?

Use external success to reduce unknowns—not to claim guaranteed transferability.

---

# 5. Hard gates

Before a niche enters the primary execution lane, answer:

1. **Who exactly pays?** Named role/owner type, business size and geography.
2. **What painful event triggers purchase?**
3. **What measurable outcome is being sold?**
4. **What is one recovered/created outcome worth?**
5. **How many qualified prospects exist?**
6. **Can we obtain/reach a list of them?**
7. **What customer channel dominates the buying/service workflow?**
8. **What system of record is used now, and what switching/integration friction exists?**
9. **What do they currently use instead?**
10. **Which successful comparable operators prove current demand and pricing?**
11. **Why is the DRF offer materially better, more specialised or more transferable?**
12. **Can it be deployed repeatedly without becoming bespoke consulting?**
13. **Does the pain recur enough to support MRR?**
14. **Are there platform, privacy, regulatory or data-rights constraints?**
15. **What counter-evidence could invalidate the niche thesis?**

If #1, #2, #5 or #6 cannot be answered, the niche is not launch-ready.

For UAE service businesses, if WhatsApp is not the proposed primary customer channel, document evidence for why another channel dominates.

---

# 6. Required niche hierarchy

Research must progressively narrow rather than stop at a broad industry:

`market → vertical → sub-niche → geography → firmographic ICP → trigger/problem → measurable outcome/product`

Example:

`construction → specialist contractors → drywall/gypsum/false ceilings → Dubai/UAE → owner-led SMEs with frequent inbound quote requests → slow/manual quotation → Instant Quote + Quote-to-Cash`

Another:

`hospitality → short-term rentals → multi-unit holiday-home operators/property managers → Dubai → operators with sufficient direct-brand demand and repeat guests → OTA dependence + fragmented guest CRM → compliant direct-booking website + CRM/lifecycle system`

Only after this sequence is clear should the implementation stack be chosen.

---

# 7. Outcome/product × niche matrix

The same niche must be scored separately for each outcome/product when fit differs.

Examples:

- HVAC maintenance × Revenue Recovery;
- HVAC maintenance × AI Voice;
- HVAC service contractors × Enquiry-to-Revenue Control;
- drywall installers × Instant Quote;
- drywall installers × Revenue Recovery;
- dental implants × Missed Lead Conversion;
- holiday-home operators × Direct Booking + CRM.

Do not conclude that a niche is universally attractive because it fits one product.

Do not conclude that one delivery vendor is universally required. The same Business × Niche may be delivered through different channels, systems of record and agent layers.

## Matrix process

For each parent opportunity:

1. generate 20–50 plausible vertical/sub-niche candidates where useful;
2. remove obvious poor fits using pain, payer, volume and reachability;
3. score the strongest candidates;
4. research the top 10 deeply enough to separate real from superficial fits;
5. identify the top 3 for commercial validation;
6. select one beachhead niche;
7. preserve the remaining ranked list for later expansion.

Avoid hard-coding one niche into the parent business opportunity.

---

# 8. Architecture fit after niche selection

Once a niche is strong enough to underwrite, define:

```text
Outcome
× Niche
× Customer Channel
× System of Record
× Agent Layer
```

## Customer channel

Choose the channel the niche already uses: WhatsApp, voice, email, web chat/forms, social messaging or another channel.

## System of record

Choose one canonical home for contacts, opportunities, lifecycle stages, consent, attribution, bookings and payments. Examples include HighLevel, HubSpot or the client's existing CRM/ERP/PMS/DMS.

Do not replace a capable incumbent system merely to force a preferred platform. The outcome may operate as an overlay/integration.

## Deterministic automation

Keep calculations, state transitions, routing, scheduled sequences, validation and other predictable work deterministic where possible.

## Agent layer

Use an agent where judgement, research, cross-system work, exceptions or orchestration materially improve the outcome. Models and vendors remain replaceable.

## UAE service-business default

`WhatsApp first → CRM/system of record → deterministic automation → native AI → external agent where needed`

HighLevel can own WhatsApp and CRM where simplicity dominates. Another messaging layer can be separated where portability or direct agent control creates measurable value. HubSpot or an incumbent CRM may remain the system of record where better suited.

Canonical rationale: `knowledge/architecture/outcome-first-modular-revenue-architecture.md`.

# 9. Offer and GTM handoff

Niche selection is not complete when a score is assigned.

For the selected beachhead niche, the next layer must produce:

- measurable promise/outcome;
- inclusions/exclusions;
- recommended setup and recurring price;
- optional upsells/usage/commission/licence structure;
- first-10-customer acquisition path;
- first-100-customer path where defensible;
- outbound, paid, SEO/content, partnerships, referrals, marketplaces/directories as relevant;
- funnel and sales-cycle assumptions;
- onboarding, support and time-to-value;
- smallest viable delivery architecture;
- Revenue Blueprint Score and economics;
- DRF Stage and next proof milestone.

Reverse-engineer successful operators first, then adapt the best supported pattern.

# 10. Initial hypotheses retained from Issue #26

These hypotheses have been converted into scored rows in `businesses/NICHES.md`. The canonical register, not this section, holds current scores.

## A. UAE HVAC / MEP contractors × Revenue Recovery

Strong hypothesis because quotations, maintenance contracts, inbound enquiries and stale opportunities have meaningful ticket value; contractor markets are identifiable; recurring leakage creates measurable ROI.

Architecture hypothesis: WhatsApp-first follow-up, CRM as system of record, deterministic reactivation sequences, agent judgement for exceptions/qualification.

Research next: company count, decision-maker reachability, average contract/quote economics, CRM penetration, WhatsApp workflow, response/follow-up benchmarks, specialised competitor density and successful comparable operators.

## B. UAE drywall / gypsum / false-ceiling installers × Instant Quote

Potentially attractive micro-vertical because scope can often be parameterised by dimensions/material/specification while faster quotation is commercially valuable.

Architecture hypothesis: WhatsApp intake + structured capture, CRM opportunity record, deterministic pricing/calculation, agent support for ambiguous scope and follow-up.

Research next: prospect volume, average job value, quotation formula standardisation, margin sensitivity, WhatsApp enquiry behaviour, reachability, successful quote-system analogues and safe pre-survey boundaries.

## C. Dubai holiday-home operators/property managers × Direct Booking + CRM

Potentially attractive because direct-booking infrastructure can combine owned website, booking, CRM, lifecycle automation and repeat-guest operations.

**Critical compliance constraint:** do not build economics around using Airbnb-provided guest data for prohibited off-platform marketing or direct repeat bookings. Design around lawful first-party acquisition, consent, owned-channel demand and platform-compliant relationships.

Research next: operator count by portfolio size, OTA commission economics, direct-booking adoption, repeat-guest rates, current PMS/channel-manager stack, CRM gaps, consent/data-rights design and comparable successful operators.

# 11. Sniper selection rule

Do not shotgun a capability across every service business.

For each opportunity:

1. generate plausible verticals;
2. narrow into commercially meaningful sub-niches;
3. score `outcome/product × niche × geography × ICP × trigger/problem`;
4. research the strongest candidates deeply;
5. find niche-specific comparable operators and counter-evidence;
6. identify customer channel and system-of-record reality;
7. contact/test the top candidates only after desk evidence;
8. choose one beachhead niche;
9. choose the minimum viable delivery architecture;
10. build one offer, setup, funnel, sales script and onboarding system;
11. clone into adjacent niches only after evidence.

Prefer domination of a small valuable category over generic presence in a huge category.

---

# 12. Relationship to DRF portfolio scoring and proof

Use the layers in this order:

```text
Business Opportunity Score
→ MRR + AI Autonomy + Evidence + Research + External Market Proof
→ Niche Attractiveness Score + Niche Evidence Confidence
→ successful-operator reverse engineering
→ offer + pricing + GTM + delivery architecture
→ Revenue Blueprint Score + economics
→ DRF Proof P0–P6 + Stage + Capital + Next Proof
→ structured V3 business case
```

The Opportunity Score selects the **vehicle/outcome**.  
The Niche Score selects the **target**.  
External Market Proof shows whether the model/category already succeeds.  
The architecture chooses **how** the outcome operates in the target.  
The Revenue Blueprint Score underwrites the selected Business × Niche.  
DRF Proof shows what **we** have actually executed and verified.  
Layer 3 makes the complete case comparable.
