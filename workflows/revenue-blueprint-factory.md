# Revenue Blueprint Factory Workflow

**Status:** Canonical workflow  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 31 August 2026  
**Governing issue:** #60  
**Name:** Revenue Blueprint Factory 7 — `RBF-7`

## Objective

Turn an idea, existing business, department or business-model pivot into one of four clear outcomes:

```text
REJECT
TEST
BUILD / SCALE
PACKAGE AS A PROOF-BACKED BLUEPRINT
```

The workflow is deliberately simple:

```text
1. CAPTURE
→ 2. UNDERWRITE
→ 3. BACKTEST
→ 4. FORWARD TEST
→ 5. LIVE PILOT
→ 6. PROVE REPEATABILITY
→ 7. SCALE AND CERTIFY
```

Each stage removes one class of uncertainty before more capital is released.

## Governing outputs

Every stage updates the same four outputs:

1. **Revenue Blueprint Score /100** — potential business quality.
2. **Proof Level P0–P6** — verified maturity.
3. **Investment Gate** — what may happen next.
4. **Return Profile** — capital, founder time, monthly cash, annual ROI, payback, runway and downside.

Canonical scoring standard:

`knowledge/guidelines/revenue-blueprint-scoring-and-investment-readiness.md`

# RBF-7 stage map

| Stage | Proof level | Core question | Required output | Gate | Default capital |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| **1. Capture** | **P0** | What is the business idea in money terms? | One-sentence business, payer, offer, revenue stream and reason to investigate | Research / Reject | $0 |
| **2. Underwrite** | **P1** | Could this structurally become a good business? | Founder summary, source-backed market/competition/pricing, initial RBS and financial model | Backtest / Reject / Hold | Desk cost only |
| **3. Backtest** | **P2** | Do the economics survive comparable/historical evidence and downside assumptions? | Three-scenario model, sensitivities, break-even, maximum loss and test hypothesis | Test / Recycle / Reject | No build beyond smallest test asset |
| **4. Forward Test** | **P3** | Will current buyers take a meaningful commercial action? | Real-market demand/pricing/channel results against pre-written threshold | Pilot / Recycle / Reject | Tier 1 up to $3,000 |
| **5. Live Pilot** | **P4** | Will someone pay, receive value and leave positive contribution? | Payment, acquisition cost, delivery, outcome, actual costs and customer evidence | Fund / Recycle / Reject | Within Tier 1; Tier 2 unlock only after pass |
| **6. Prove Repeatability** | **P5** | Can the same core system work repeatedly without founder heroics? | At least three independent customers/cohorts/cycles, positive contribution, bounded time and standard delivery | Scale / Hold / Recycle | Tier 2: +$7,000; $10,000 cumulative |
| **7. Scale and Certify** | **P6** | Can acquisition, delivery, margin and retention scale, and can another operator reproduce it? | Scale evidence, capacity, risk controls and complete Blueprint package | Blueprint Certified / Continue / Stop scale | Tier 3: +$20,000; $30,000 cumulative |

# Stage 1 — Capture

## Required answers

- What are we selling?
- Who pays?
- Why will they pay?
- What is the first/core revenue stream?
- Is the revenue upfront, recurring, royalty, commission, usage or another plain type?
- Why now?
- What would make us reject it immediately?

## Output

```text
We sell <offer/outcome> to <payer> for <price/basis>
because <pain/value>. Revenue arrives as <type>.
```

## Gate

- **GO TO UNDERWRITE** when buyer, problem and possible payment are coherent.
- **KILL** when there is no identifiable payer or revenue mechanism.
- **HOLD** when the timing depends on an external change.

Do not score a vague idea.

# Stage 2 — Underwrite

## Required work

Use:

- `knowledge/templates/business-opportunity-readme.md`
- `knowledge/templates/business-opportunity-research.md`
- the correct model-specific benchmark card.

Research:

- customer and payer;
- pain and willingness to pay;
- offer and revenue streams;
- current pricing and substitutes;
- demand, market and timing;
- acquisition channels and ads;
- delivery, human work and AI/provider costs;
- unit economics;
- recurring revenue and retention logic;
- capital, risk and dependency;
- first 12-month financial model.

## Required output

- initial Revenue Blueprint Score;
- P1 label;
- downside/base/upside financial assumptions;
- top three assumptions that control the result;
- one fatal-risk question;
- proposed backtest.

## Gate

Proceed only when:

- the money model is understandable in 20 seconds;
- at least one credible payer and channel exist;
- the downside is bounded;
- no unresolved legal/platform condition makes the business impossible;
- the backtest can be performed.

A high score does not unlock investment here.

# Stage 3 — Backtest

## Objective

Test the proposed business against existing facts before spending on the market.

## Backtest sources

Use the strongest available:

- the client's own historical sales, leads, costs and delivery data;
- DRF/Talent Bridge/iMPLEMENTAi operating data;
- competitor pricing and traffic;
- marketplace sales and review data;
- advertising keyword/CPC and audience data;
- public company/unit-economics data;
- cohort or category benchmarks;
- analogous failed businesses and counter-evidence.

## Required stress tests

Change the three assumptions that most affect cash:

- demand/conversion;
- price/ARPU/take rate;
- CAC;
- churn/refunds;
- gross margin or compute/provider cost;
- founder/delivery hours;
- platform/commission/royalty rules.

At minimum model:

```text
DOWNSIDE · BASE · UPSIDE
```

## Pass conditions

The opportunity reaches P2 when:

- assumptions and evidence are traceable;
- break-even and maximum loss are visible;
- downside does not create an unacceptable/fatal exposure;
- there is a credible route to positive contribution;
- the next commercial uncertainty can be tested for $3,000 or less;
- a forward test has a pre-written hypothesis, metric and threshold.

## Gate

- **GO TO TEST** — economics justify a bounded market test.
- **RECYCLE** — change offer, price, niche or channel, then backtest again.
- **KILL** — economics fail under reasonable assumptions.

# Stage 4 — Forward Test

## Objective

Measure current buyer behaviour before committing to full delivery or scale.

## Test Card

Write this before launch:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
<what must be true>

TEST
<smallest real-market action>

METRIC
<cash or behaviour measured>

PASS THRESHOLD
<exact number/date>

FAIL / RECYCLE THRESHOLD
<exact number/date>

MAXIMUM BUDGET
<= $3,000

FOUNDER TIMEBOX
<hours and end date>
```

## Suitable demand evidence

From weak to strong:

```text
interview answer
→ qualified reply/demo request
→ signed LOI
→ checkout/start action
→ deposit/preorder
→ full payment
```

Use the strongest evidence practical for the model.

## Digital-business test examples

- paid landing page with measured qualified conversion;
- direct offer to a defined warm/cold buyer list;
- preorder or deposit campaign;
- paid concierge service before software build;
- marketplace listing with real checkout;
- manual fulfilment of the promised outcome;
- price test with consistent offer and audience.

## Pass conditions

P3 requires:

- a real defined audience;
- a consistent offer and price;
- observed behaviour, not agent opinion;
- cost and conversion captured;
- pre-written threshold met or a clearly documented recycle decision.

## Gate

- **PILOT** when demand/pricing signal passes.
- **RECYCLE** when a specific variable can be changed and retested.
- **KILL** when buyers repeatedly reject the problem/price after a valid test.

# Stage 5 — Live Pilot

## Objective

Prove the full commercial loop:

```text
acquire
→ collect cash
→ deliver
→ create value
→ measure cost
→ retain contribution
```

## Required evidence

- exact cash collected;
- acquisition source and cost;
- buyer and use case;
- sale-to-first-value time;
- direct platform/payment/provider/AI costs;
- founder and team hours;
- failures, support, refunds and recovery;
- customer outcome;
- gross and contribution margin;
- what must change before customer two.

## Pass conditions

P4 requires one genuine paid use with delivered/activated value and reconciled economics.

## Gate

- **FUND** only when the pilot proves payment, useful delivery and no fatal economics.
- **RECYCLE** if the customer paid but offer/delivery economics require a bounded redesign.
- **KILL** if the outcome cannot be delivered economically or safely.

---

# Stage 6 — Prove Repeatability

## Objective

Prove that the business is a system, not a founder performance.

## Minimum pattern proof

- same target customer class;
- materially same core offer and price logic;
- materially same acquisition and delivery process;
- at least three independent paid customers, cohorts or commercial cycles;
- positive contribution across the pattern, not only the best customer;
- customer outcome measured;
- founder hours per unit stable or falling;
- failure/recovery steps documented;
- no undisclosed custom work carrying the result.

For high-volume digital products, use cohort evidence rather than three transactions.

## Tier 2 use of funds

The additional **$7,000** to a **$10,000 cumulative** commitment may be used for:

- repeatable acquisition;
- delivery tooling;
- automation after the manual process is understood;
- customer-success/retention controls;
- legal/commercial standardisation;
- limited working capital.

## Pass conditions

P5 requires:

- repeated paid acquisition;
- repeated successful delivery;
- positive contribution;
- known CAC/payback or verified non-paid channel economics;
- bounded founder dependency;
- a 12-month model updated from actuals.

## Gate

- **SCALE** when the repeated system and economics hold.
- **HOLD** when demand is seasonal or an external dependency limits timing.
- **RECYCLE** when one bottleneck can be redesigned.

# Stage 7 — Scale and Blueprint Certification

## Objective

Increase volume without destroying customer outcomes, margin, cash or operator freedom; then package the proven business so another competent operator can reproduce it.

## Scale test

Test one controlled increase at a time:

- paid acquisition budget;
- audience/channel volume;
- delivery capacity;
- automation;
- geography/niche;
- price/packaging;
- partner distribution.

Measure incremental return on the **next** dollar, not only historic average ROI.

## Tier 3 use of funds

The additional **$20,000** to a **$30,000 cumulative** commitment may be used for:

- the proven acquisition channel;
- capacity and working capital;
- automation and monitoring;
- reliability, quality and support;
- documentation and Blueprint packaging;
- reserve for measured downside.

## P6 pass conditions

- acquisition remains economic at higher volume;
- delivery quality and time-to-value hold;
- retention/repeat behaviour is visible where relevant;
- contribution and cash remain healthy;
- capacity limit and next constraint are known;
- founder dependency is controlled;
- another competent operator can follow the operating package;
- the Blueprint specification and evidence register are complete.

## Certification output

Use:

`knowledge/templates/revenue-blueprint-specification.md`

Certification must state:

- Proof Level and evidence period;
- sample/customer/cohort size;
- Revenue Blueprint Score;
- capital and founder-time requirements;
- monthly/annual return scenarios;
- known failure modes;
- no guaranteed earnings claim.

# Client business benchmark route

Use the same workflow for an existing client business.

## Start with actuals

Collect at least 12 months where available:

- revenue by stream;
- gross profit and direct costs;
- leads, conversion, average sale and sales cycle;
- CAC/marketing spend by channel;
- retention/repeat purchase/churn;
- delivery capacity and labour hours;
- cash collection and working-capital timing;
- customer and channel concentration;
- platform/vendor dependency;
- owner dependency.

## Benchmark output

```text
CURRENT BUSINESS
Revenue Blueprint Score + P-level + Return Profile

BOTTLENECK
The factor most constraining cash/value

BASELINE
Current monthly numbers

TEST
One bounded improvement experiment

PIVOT / ADD-ON
Compare the existing model with alternative revenue streams using the same scoring system
```

Do not advise a pivot until the current business is measured and the alternative is backtested.

---

# Department benchmark route

For every department:

1. classify its economic job: create revenue, protect revenue, improve margin, accelerate cash or enable future value;
2. record annual cost, outputs, quality, cycle time and verified economic value;
3. calculate conservative department ROI;
4. identify one bottleneck;
5. run one improvement Test Card;
6. retain only improvements that produce measurable value.

Do not double-count the same company revenue across departments.

# Required factory artefacts

Each active opportunity folder should ultimately contain:

```text
businesses/<opportunity>/
├── README.md                 # founder summary
├── RESEARCH.md               # underwriting and evidence
├── financial-model.xlsx      # current monthly return model
├── investment-memo.md        # capital decision
├── blueprint.md              # operating recipe/certification
└── evidence/                 # actual tests and proof where needed
```

Create files only when their stage requires them. P0 ideas do not need the full package.

## Factory rule

> **Spend the smallest money and founder time necessary to remove the next biggest uncertainty. Only scale what has produced cash, customer value and repeatable contribution.**
